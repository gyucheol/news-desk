# AI / Deep-Learning Speech Enhancement for Cleaning and "Polishing" Spoken-Voice Recordings (methods, models, metrics, failure modes — state of the field as of Oct 2026)

*Source-type labels used below: **[Paper]** = what a peer-reviewed paper / arXiv preprint states; **[Challenge]** = official challenge overview or analysis paper written by the organizers; **[Official doc]** = vendor technical/developer documentation; **[Vendor claim]** = vendor marketing or blog claims (not independently verified); **[3rd-party]** = press/secondary reporting. Everything under "Inferences" is my own reasoning, not a source claim. Most arXiv papers below were read in full text (PDF), not just abstracts; quotes are verbatim unless marked "paraphrase".*

## Q1. Discriminative / mask-based denoising: how networks estimate time-frequency masks or clean spectra (RNNoise, PercepNet, DeepFilterNet, Demucs "Denoiser"), and how they are trained

### Takeaway
"Predictive" (discriminative) enhancers learn one deterministic mapping from noisy to clean speech: most predict a per-band or per-bin gain ("mask") that is multiplied onto the noisy short-time spectrum (RNNoise, PercepNet, DeepFilterNet), while others output the clean waveform directly (Demucs Denoiser). All are trained on synthetic pairs made by mixing clean speech with noise, and often room impulse responses, at random levels. What they add over classic DSP is learned suppression of non-stationary noise (babble, keyboard, dogs) without hand-tuned voice-activity and noise-tracking estimators. Their structural limit is that they mostly attenuate or reshape what is already in the input, and they trade residual noise against speech distortion.

### Cited Findings
**What classic DSP does and what deep learning replaces**
- [Paper] Classic noise suppressors chain a voice activity detector, a noise spectral estimator and spectral subtraction. "Each of the 3 components requires accurate estimators and are difficult to tune." RNNoise uses deep learning "to replace the estimators that have traditionally been hard to correctly tune." — [Valin, RNNoise, arXiv 1709.08243](https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.08243)
- [Paper] Classic estimator-based methods "still have trouble dealing with commons noises such as non-stationary noise or a babble noise", whereas DNNs "perform significantly better on non-stationary noise and babble noise". — [Défossez et al., arXiv 2006.12847](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.12847)
- [Challenge] "DNN based methods are shown to outperform conventional SE techniques in suppressing nonstationary noises." — [Reddy et al., Interspeech 2020 DNS Challenge, arXiv 2005.13981](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.13981)
- [Paper] The classic STFT trade-off: "long windows can cause musical noise and reverb-like effects, whereas short windows do not provide sufficient frequency resolution for removing noise between pitch harmonics." — [Valin et al., PercepNet, arXiv 2008.04259](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.04259)

**Mask concepts**
- [Paper] Traditional methods output "either an enhanced version of the magnitude spectrum or ... an estimate of the ideal binary mask (IBM)". — [arXiv 2006.12847](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.12847)
- [Paper] RNNoise's band gains "are simply the square root of the ideal ratio mask (IRM)" and are bounded to [0, 1]. — [arXiv 1709.08243](https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.08243)
- [Paper] Magnitude-mask approaches "require ISTFT or other methods to obtain waveform from predictions, which often produce audible artifacts due to missing or mismatching phase." — [Su, Jin, Finkelstein, HiFi-GAN, arXiv 2006.05694](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.05694)
- [Paper] Conv-TasNet lists "the decoupling of the phase and magnitude of the signal" as a drawback of time-frequency methods and instead applies masks to a learned time-domain basis. — [Luo & Mesgarani, arXiv 1809.07454](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.07454)
- [Paper] "Deep filtering" estimates a complex multi-frame filter per frequency bin, Y(t) = w(t)^H x̄(t), over the current and neighbouring STFT frames, to exploit short-time speech correlations. The authors state "deep filtering outperforms the mostly used complex ratio mask (CRM)". — [Schröter et al., DeepFilterNet3, arXiv 2305.08227](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.08227)

**RNNoise (Jean-Marc Valin, Mozilla; MMSP 2018; hybrid DSP + RNN)**
- [Paper] 48 kHz full-band. 20 ms windows with 50% overlap (10 ms hop). Spectrum split into 22 triangular Bark-like bands (the Opus layout), so the network outputs only 22 gains. A pitch comb filter "attenuates noise between pitch harmonics", which the coarse bands cannot resolve. There are 42 input features (22 Bark cepstral coefficients plus derivatives, pitch-correlation DCT, pitch period, and a non-stationarity metric). — [arXiv 1709.08243](https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.08243)
- [Paper] The network is GRU-based, with 215 units, 4 hidden layers and an auxiliary voice-activity output. It has 87,503 weights (8-bit quantizable), costs about 40 Mflops in total, and uses about 1.3% of one Haswell i7 core or 14% of a Raspberry Pi 3 core. — [arXiv 1709.08243](https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.08243)
- [Paper] Training data is built synthetically by adding noise to clean speech: the McGill TSP speech database (French and English) and the NTT Multi-Lingual Speech Database (21 languages), plus noise from fans, office, crowd, airplane, car, train and construction. 6 h of speech and 4 h of noise were turned into 140 h of noisy speech through random gains, random second-order filters (for robustness to microphone response) and resampling to 40–54 kHz. Clean-only and noise-only segments are included. — [arXiv 1709.08243](https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.08243)
- [Paper] The loss L = (g^γ − ĝ^γ)² "determines how the network weighs excessive attenuation versus insufficient attenuation". γ→0 "would make the suppression too aggressive", and γ = 1/2 is the chosen trade-off. Without smoothing, the output "can sometimes sound overly dry", so gain decay is limited (equivalent to a 135 ms reverberation time). — [arXiv 1709.08243](https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.08243)
- [Paper] PESQ results improved over the SpeexDSP MMSE suppressor "especially for non-stationary noise types". A demo and BSD-licensed code are at [people.xiph.org/~jm/demo/rnnoise](https://people.xiph.org/~jm/demo/rnnoise/) and [github.com/xiph/rnnoise](https://github.com/xiph/rnnoise). — [arXiv 1709.08243](https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.08243)

**PercepNet (Valin, Isik, Phansalkar, Giri, Helwani, Krishnaswamy — Amazon Web Services; Interspeech 2020)**
- [Paper] It models perception of the spectral envelope and periodicity using 34 ERB-spaced bands from 0 to 20 kHz. It "operates on 10-ms frames with 40 ms of look-ahead and can enhance 48 kHz speech in real time using just 4.1% of an x86 CPU core." It uses a new acausal pitch comb filter whose strength the DNN learns, plus an "envelope postfilter" modelled on CELP codec formant postfilters. — [arXiv 2008.04259](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.04259)
- [Paper] "Not enough [comb] filtering results in roughness, whereas too much results in a robotic voice." — [arXiv 2008.04259](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.04259)
- [Paper] Training used synthetic mixtures: 120 h of 48 kHz clean speech (more than 200 speakers, more than 20 languages) and 80 h of noise, convolved with simulated and measured room impulse responses. The target keeps early reflections "so that only late reverberation is attenuated". — [arXiv 2008.04259](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.04259)
- [Paper] On the DNS Challenge's official P.808 MOS (synthetic without reverb / synthetic with reverb / real recordings), Noisy scored 3.32 / 2.78 / 2.97, the baseline 3.49 / 2.64 / 3.00, and PercepNet 3.92 / 3.16 / 3.51. — [arXiv 2008.04259](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.04259)

**DeepFilterNet 1/2/3 (Schröter et al., FAU Erlangen-Nürnberg and WS Audiology; ICASSP 2022, IWAENC 2022, Interspeech 2023 demo)**
- [Paper] There are two stages: (1) 32 ERB-scaled real gains reconstruct the coarse speech envelope; (2) a complex 5-tap deep filter is applied only to the lowest 96 bins (up to about 4.8 kHz), where voiced/periodic energy lies. It runs at 48 kHz with 20 ms windows, a 10 ms hop and 2 frames of look-ahead, "resulting in an overall latency of 40 ms". — [arXiv 2305.08227](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.08227)
- [Paper] The model predicts the local SNR. If ξ < −10 dB it disables both decoders and returns a "silent spectrum"; if ξ > 20 dB it skips the deep-filter stage. Real-time factor is 0.19 single-threaded on a notebook i5-8250U CPU, implemented in Rust with the tract inference engine, with a LADSPA plugin for PipeWire virtual microphones. — [arXiv 2305.08227](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.08227); code at [github.com/Rikorose/DeepFilterNet](https://github.com/Rikorose/DeepFilterNet)
- [Paper] It was trained on the full multilingual DNS4 dataset, with PTDB and VCTK oversampled ×10. On the VoiceBank+DEMAND test, PESQ went 2.81 → 3.08 → 3.17 (DFN → DFN2 → DFN3) while STOI went 0.942 → 0.943 → 0.944. — [arXiv 2305.08227](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.08227)

**Meta/FAIR "Denoiser" (Défossez, Synnaeve, Adi; Interspeech 2020; causal Demucs)**
- [Paper] It is waveform-to-waveform: a causal Demucs convolutional encoder/decoder with U-Net skips and an LSTM. The architecture "was initially developed for music source separation". Training uses an L1 waveform loss plus a multi-resolution STFT loss. — [arXiv 2006.12847](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.12847); code at [github.com/facebookresearch/denoiser](https://github.com/facebookresearch/denoiser)
- [Paper] Data: Valentini (VoiceBank+DEMAND) and DNS 2020. Augmentations: Remix (shuffling noises within a batch), BandMask (a SpecAugment-like band-stop), "Revecho" (synthetic decaying echoes with RT60 0.3–1.3 s) and random shifts. — [arXiv 2006.12847](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.12847)
- [Paper] A "dry/wet knob" outputs dry·x + (1−dry)·ŷ. "A small amount of bleeding (5%) improves the overall perceived quality." — [arXiv 2006.12847](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.12847)
- [Paper] As an ASR front-end without retraining the ASR model, it recovered up to 51% of the WER lost to noise at 0 dB SNR, and on average 41% (dev-clean) and 31% (dev-other). — [arXiv 2006.12847](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.12847)

**Training-data practice and its pitfalls**
- [Challenge] DNS 2020 clean speech came from LibriVox audiobooks (11,350 speakers, mostly English). The organizers released a synthetic test set plus real recordings. — [arXiv 2005.13981](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.13981)
- [Challenge] DNS 2021 expanded the clean training set to more than 760 h "including singing voice, emotion data, and non-English languages" (e.g., Mandarin, German, Spanish; the paper gives 185.41 h of Mandarin in one place and "170+ hours" in another). Earlier data "only included the clips in the English language" and lacked "crying, yelling, laughter or singing". — [Reddy et al., Interspeech 2021 DNS, arXiv 2101.01902](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.01902)
- [Challenge] Label noise exists "even in various 'high-quality' speech corpora", so "most SE studies are implicitly conducting a sort of noisy-target training". Declared and effective bandwidths often mismatch: about 25% of LibriTTS, about 100% of DNS5 LibriVox and about 100% of CommonVoice 11.0 English. "Both issues can cause unexpected model behaviors." — [Zhang et al., Lessons from URGENT 2024, arXiv 2506.01611](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.01611)

### Inferences
- A pure gain mask can only scale energy that is already present. Mask-based models therefore cannot by themselves restore missing high frequencies, clipped peaks or codec-erased phonemes. This is why the "polish to studio quality" use case moved to generative models (Q3). The point generalizes Miipher's statement that a missing phoneme "introduces an unrecoverable error" (Q3).
- Every system exposes some form of the over-suppression vs residual-noise trade-off: RNNoise's γ, Demucs's dry/wet mix, DeepFilterNet's thresholds and attenuation limits. Consumer "strength"/"mix" sliders are plausibly this knob, but no product documentation found confirms the mapping.
- DeepFilterNet's documented rule (local SNR below −10 dB returns silence) implies that speech buried in very loud noise can be muted rather than recovered. That is a design-level form of over-suppression.

### Gaps
- PercepNet and DeepFilterNet 1/2 training hyperparameters (SNR ranges, RIR counts) were not extracted. The DFN1/DFN2 papers were not read in full.
- There is no head-to-head published comparison of these four models on the same real-world podcast/voice-memo material. Benchmark numbers come from different test sets.

## Q2. Neural dereverberation and "dialogue isolation" (separating a voice from room echo, background music or other speakers)

### Takeaway
Dereverberation uses the same supervised recipe: dry or early-reflection targets paired with inputs convolved with measured or simulated room impulse responses. Separating a voice from other voices or music uses source separation (Conv-TasNet, Demucs, three-stem "cocktail fork" models for speech/music/effects) or target-speaker extraction, which uses a clue such as a 10–30 s enrollment clip, lip video or direction. Overlapping speech and strong reverberation remain the hardest conditions, and personalized or isolating models documentedly sometimes suppress the wanted talker or leave dereverberation artifacts.

### Cited Findings
- [Paper] Conv-TasNet: a linear time-domain encoder, masks estimated by a temporal convolutional network, and a linear decoder. It "surpasses several ideal time-frequency magnitude masks in two-speaker speech separation" and has "a shorter minimum latency". — [Luo & Mesgarani, arXiv 1809.07454](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.07454)
- [Paper] Target speech extraction isolates one speaker from a multi-talker mixture using clues: spatial direction, video of the lips, or "a pre-recorded enrollment utterance from which their voice characteristics can be derived". The core difficulty is that "target and non-target speech signals share similar characteristics". — [Žmolíková et al., IEEE Signal Processing Magazine overview, arXiv 2301.13341](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.13341)
- [Paper] The "Cocktail Fork Problem" (Petermann, Wichern, Wang, Le Roux; ICASSP 2022) separates a soundtrack into "speech, music, and sound effects". Its Divide and Remaster (DnR) dataset (LibriSpeech + FMA + FSD50K) mimics professional mixes "in terms of source overlap and relative loudness". The best model achieved SI-SDR improvements of 11.2 dB (speech), 11.0 dB (music) and 10.8 dB (effects). — [arXiv 2110.09958](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.09958)
- [Paper] "Demucs is an open-source music separation model capable of separating speech from background music". It and VoiceFixer "have been used for dataset cleansing". — [Nakata et al., Sidon, arXiv 2509.17052](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.17052)
- [Challenge] The ICASSP 2023 (5th) DNS Challenge targeted "joint denosing, dereverberation and suppression of interfering talkers". Each test clip came with 10–30 s of enrollment speech for "personalized" models. Headset acoustics (e.g., direct-to-reverberant ratio, SNR) "make it possible to suppress neighboring talkers even without enrollment data". — [Dubey et al., arXiv 2303.11510](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11510)
- [Challenge] Same challenge, conclusion: "Results show degradation in signal quality (SIG) for most of the models ... Personalized model end up suppressing the primary talker. Noticeable SIG degradation's happen due to suppression of primary talker's speech and/or leakage of interfering talker and noise." — [arXiv 2303.11510](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11510)
- [Paper] Demucs Denoiser at DNS 2020: the "remove reverb" model had "poor performance on the Reverb category due to dereverberation artifacts". Keeping about 10% of the reverb improved ratings on synthetic data "but not for real recordings", while simulating reverb with "two sources" improved real-recording ratings. — [arXiv 2006.12847](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.12847)
- [Paper] HiFi-GAN (Su et al.) training data was made by convolving DAPS studio speech with 270 measured impulse responses from the MIT Impulse Response Survey and adding noise from the REVERB and ACE challenge databases, with extra impulse responses used for "equalization distortion". The authors use a spectrogram-domain discriminator because "it's also easier to identify long-span reverberation residual in the time-frequency domain". — [arXiv 2006.05694](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.05694)
- [Paper] A diffusion model (SGMSE+) used for single-channel dereverberation "does not produce any of the vocalized artifacts observed in the speech enhancement experiments for low input SNRs". — [Richter et al., arXiv 2208.05830](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05830)
- [Paper] Full dereverberation can sound unnatural. PercepNet notes that over headphones "the lack of any reverberation can make the enhanced signal sound overly dry and unnatural" [arXiv 2008.04259](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.04259). Google's ReverbMiipher (2025) notes "traditional Speech Restoration (SR) usually completely removes reverberation" and proposes a ReverbEncoder that removes noise "while retaining the original reverberation characteristics", with controllable reverb. — [Nakata et al., arXiv 2505.05077](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.05077)
- [Challenge] "Overlapped speech, strong wideband / instantaneous noise, and high reverberation remain the hardest challenges in SE tasks." SNR is a poor difficulty measure: "conventional metrics such as signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) are often misleading." — [arXiv 2506.01611](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.01611)
- [Official doc] NVIDIA's Audio Effects SDK lists "Speaker Focus", which "identifies and isolates the prominent speaker by removing all background speakers from the input audio". It also lists a combined "Denoise and Dereverb" effect, documented as performing better than applying the two effects separately (paraphrase). — [NVIDIA AFX SDK User Guide](https://docs.nvidia.com/maxine/afx/latest/)
- [Vendor claim] Krisp says its lite voice-isolation model "performs background voice and noise cancellation with the primary speaker detected by speaker-to-microphone proximity cues". — [Krisp blog, updated Dec 2025](https://krisp.ai/blog/small-voice-isolation-model/)

### Inferences
- "Dialogue isolation" in editors is technically a speech-vs-everything-else separation, close to the cocktail-fork three-stem task. Whether any product uses Demucs-, Conv-TasNet- or TSE-style models is not publicly documented in the sources found.
- Removing a second voice that is not marked as the "target" requires a cue (enrollment, proximity, direction). Without a cue, a model cannot know which of two similar voices to keep. This explains the documented failure where personalized/isolation models suppress the primary talker.
- Fully dry output is a stylistic artifact of its own. Partial dereverberation (Demucs, PercepNet's early-reflection target, ReverbMiipher) is the documented compromise.

### Gaps
- No primary technical documentation was found for how Apple, Adobe or video-editor "dialogue isolation" features separate voice from music. That is a product question, but the algorithm is undocumented.
- No quantitative study found of dereverberation artifacts on real (not simulated) podcast or lecture recordings beyond the DNS real-recording MOS tables.

## Q3. Generative / resynthesis-based enhancement ("speech restoration"): HiFi-GAN, HiFi-GAN-2, VoiceFixer, Miipher(-2), Resemble Enhance, SGMSE+/StoRM, and 2024–2026 work

### Takeaway
Generative restorers do not mask the input. They synthesize a new waveform from a learned model of clean studio speech, conditioned on the degraded input. The main routes are:

- GAN waveform generators: HiFi-GAN, FINALLY.
- "Parametric resynthesis": predict clean features (MFCC, mel, or self-supervised w2v-BERT/USM/WavLM features), then render them with a neural vocoder. Examples are HiFi-GAN-2, VoiceFixer, Miipher, Miipher-2, Sidon and UniPASE.
- Diffusion or flow-matching sampling: SGMSE+, StoRM, UNIVERSE, Resemble Enhance's flow-matching (CFM) enhancer.
- Token language-model or masked generative models: MaskSR, AnyEnhance, LLaSE-G1.

Because they regenerate content, they can extend bandwidth (e.g., 16 → 48 kHz), undo clipping, codec damage and EQ coloration, and make phone or web audio sound "studio-like". The cost is documented hallucination of phonemes or words and of speaker traits, language dependency, run-to-run randomness, and much higher compute or latency. Challenges in 2025–2026 were won by discriminative or hybrid systems, not purely generative ones.

### Cited Findings
**HiFi-GAN (Su, Jin, Finkelstein — Princeton and Adobe Research; Interspeech 2020)**
- [Paper] The goal is to transform recorded speech so it sounds "as though it had been recorded in a studio", addressing noise, reverberation and equalization distortion jointly. It uses an "end-to-end feed-forward WaveNet architecture, trained with multi-scale adversarial discriminators in both the time domain and the time-frequency domain", plus deep feature matching losses. — [arXiv 2006.05694](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.05694)
- [Paper] Earlier waveform-domain methods "have distortions of their own" and were found "sensitive to training data and difficult to generalize to unfamiliar noises and reverberation". — [arXiv 2006.05694](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.05694)

**HiFi-GAN-2 (Su, Jin, Finkelstein; WASPAA 2021)**
- [Paper/Official doc] It has three stages: "a recurrent network predicts the acoustic features (MFCCs) of a clean signal"; then "a feed-forward WaveNet (modeled via multi-domain multi-scale adversarial training) generates a clean 16kHz signal"; then a pretrained bandwidth-extension network upsamples to 48 kHz. It targets consumer recordings with moderate noise, reverb and EQ distortion, and listening tests on TED Talks and VoxCeleb indicate quality "close to studio quality on real-world speech content". — [Adobe Research publication page](https://research.adobe.com/publication/hifi-gan-2-studio-quality-speech-enhancement-via-generative-adversarial-networks-conditioned-on-acoustic-features); project page: [Princeton PIXL](https://pixl.cs.princeton.edu/pubs/Su_2021_HSS/)
- [Paper] FINALLY's authors later note HiFi-GAN-2 "primarily work[s] at the full waveform resolution, similar to WaveNet", which makes it computationally heavier. They also say neither HiFi-GAN-2's nor UNIVERSE's code was released ("the authors did not release any code"). — [Babaev et al., FINALLY, arXiv 2410.05920](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.05920)

**VoiceFixer (Liu et al., ByteDance SAMI and collaborators; arXiv 2021)**
- [Paper] It frames "general speech restoration" as removing several distortions at once: noise, reverberation, low resolution and clipping. The analysis stage is a ResUNet that restores the mel spectrogram (by mask estimation on the mel spectrogram). The synthesis stage is a TFGAN neural vocoder. Training used VCTK, AISHELL-3 and "HQ-TTS" speech, "VD-Noise" noise and a simulated "RIR-44k" set. Reported MOS gains were +0.499 for the general model over single-task models and +0.256 more for VoiceFixer. The authors suggest potential for "restoring old movies and historical speeches". — [arXiv 2109.13731](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.13731)
- [Paper] Specialised single-task models (Dereverb-UNet, Declip-UNet) "achieve the highest scores" on dereverberation and declipping, with the general model "slightly worse". — [arXiv 2109.13731](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.13731)
- [Official doc / README] It handles "noise, reveberation, low resolution (2kHz~44.1kHz) and clipping (0.1-1.0 threshold) effect within one model", with a 44.1 kHz "universal speaker-independent neural vocoder". "Mode 2" "might work sometimes on seriously degraded real speech". MIT license. — [github.com/haoheliu/voicefixer](https://github.com/haoheliu/voicefixer)

**Miipher (Google; WASPAA 2023)**
- [Paper] Purpose: convert web-collected speech to studio quality to create TTS training data. Inputs are w2v-BERT self-supervised features plus PnG-BERT text features from the transcript, plus a speaker embedding ("since SSL features often lose speaker information"). A WaveFit vocoder outputs 24 kHz audio. — [Koizumi et al., arXiv 2303.01664](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.01664)
- [Paper] Documented failure cases that motivated the design: "Phoneme masking: Speech signals are sometimes masked by noise and/or reverberation"; "Phoneme deletion: Important frequency components of some phonemes could be missing from the signal due to codecs and/or down-sampling. If a noisy sample lacks a phoneme, it introduces an unrecoverable error." The fix treats the problem "as a text-conditioned speech inpainting". The paper also states: "If SR fails to restore speech samples and produces artifacts, they will negatively affect the subsequent training". — [arXiv 2303.01664](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.01664)

**Miipher-2 (Google DeepMind; 2025)**
- [Paper] It runs without conditioning (no transcript or speaker ID), using a frozen Universal Speech Model ("supporting over 300 languages") with parallel adapters and a WaveFit vocoder. It was trained on 3,195 h from 1,642 speakers across 44 languages (54 locales). Real-time factor is 0.0078 on a TPU, enough to process "a million-hour speech dataset in approximately three days" on 100 accelerators. It reports results "superior or comparable" to conventional systems in WER, speaker similarity and quality "across all tested languages". — [Karita et al., arXiv 2505.04457](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.04457)
- [Official doc] The demo page shows English, MLS languages and five "unknown-language" FLEURS locales (ca, ru, ur, sw, mi). It shows no Korean or other CJK samples. Per the fetch tool's paraphrase, it says CJK locales were covered by fine-tuning data. — [Miipher-2 demo](https://google.github.io/df-conformer/miipher2/index.html)

**Sidon (University of Tokyo; 2025, open source)**
- [Paper] An open Miipher-2-style model: w2v-BERT 2.0 (trained on 4.5M h in 143 languages) fine-tuned as a feature cleanser, plus a vocoder. It runs "up to 500 × faster than real time on a single GPU", and was evaluated on 100 of FLEURS's 102 languages using CER, NISQA, DNSMOS and speaker similarity. "Code and model are released". — [arXiv 2509.17052](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.17052)

**Resemble Enhance (Resemble AI; open-source repo)**
- [Official doc / README] It has two modules: a denoiser that "separates speech from noisy audio" and an enhancer that restores "audio distortions and extending the audio bandwidth". The enhancer is trained in two stages: an autoencoder plus vocoder, then a "latent conditional flow matching (CFM) model". Models are "trained on high-quality 44.1kHz speech data". MIT license. The README states no limitations. — [github.com/resemble-ai/resemble-enhance](https://github.com/resemble-ai/resemble-enhance)

**SGMSE+ and StoRM (Universität Hamburg; IEEE/ACM TASLP 2023)**
- [Paper] SGMSE+ is score-based diffusion driven by a stochastic differential equation. The reverse process starts "from a mixture of noisy speech and Gaussian noise" rather than pure noise, and needs about 30 reverse steps. With a predictor-corrector sampler at 60 network evaluations, the real-time factor is 1.77 on an RTX 2080 Ti GPU (30 evaluations gives 0.89; 90 gives 2.65). — [Richter et al., arXiv 2208.05830](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05830)
- [Paper] Documented artifacts: "at very low input SNRs, some 'vocalizing' artifacts with very poor articulation and no linguistic meaning are occasionally produced ... breathing sounds or speech-like sounds were generated in noisy regions where no speech was originally present." The hypothesized cause: "the score model may erroneously identify noise energy in some T-F areas as corrupted speech." — [arXiv 2208.05830](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05830)
- [Paper] StoRM: "diffusion models ... may also produce vocalizing and breathing artifacts in adverse conditions", whereas predictive models "do not produce such artifacts but tend to distort the target speech instead". StoRM first runs a predictive estimate, then uses diffusion to regenerate. That cuts compute "by an order of magnitude": about 4.5·10¹¹ MAC/s for StoRM with 20 steps vs 2.1·10¹² for the best setting of the diffusion baseline SGMSE+M (50 steps plus corrector). On ASR, that pure diffusion baseline "hardly improves the ASR performance over the noisy mixture", while StoRM improves WER by a relative 19% over it. — [Lemercier et al., arXiv 2212.11851](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.11851)

**FINALLY (Samsung Research; NeurIPS 2024)**
- [Paper] A GAN built on HiFi++ with a WavLM encoder, a WavLM-convolutional-feature perceptual loss and MS-STFT discriminators, producing 48 kHz output. Position statement: the model "should not generate new speech content but rather 'refine' existing speech as if it was recorded in ideal conditions (studio-like quality)", i.e. find "the most probable clean speech sample that retains the lexical content and voice of the original". The authors argue GANs suit this because they are drawn to that most-probable point. — [arXiv 2410.05920](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.05920)
- [Paper] Stated limitations: on "severely degraded" speech "some artefacts remain ... attributed to the high uncertainty of the initial signal, such as the speaker's voice". It "doesn't suit low latency scenarios", because WavLM needs context. Real-time factors on a V100 GPU, as extracted from the PDF's Table 2: FINALLY 0.03, VoiceFixer 0.02, Demucs 0.08, BBED 0.43, StoRM 1.05; HiFi-GAN-2 is listed at 0.50 although its code was unreleased. — [arXiv 2410.05920](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.05920)

**2025–2026 developments**
- [Paper] UniPASE (2026; accepted to IEEE TASLP) is a WavLM-derived "DeWavLM-Omni" phonetic-representation enhancer followed by an adapter, a vocoder and a 48 kHz PostNet. It distinguishes "linguistic" hallucinations (incorrect spoken content) from "acoustic" ones (inconsistent speaker characteristics). It was the backbone of an ICASSP 2026 URGENT submission that "achieved 1st place in the objective evaluation". — [Rong et al., arXiv 2604.14606](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.14606)
- [Paper] Token/LM-based restorers, as summarized in UniPASE's related work: MaskSR is a "token-based masked generative model" with the DAC codec; AnyEnhance is a masked generative model with semantic-then-acoustic stages that also does target-speaker extraction; LLaSE-G1 uses "parallel token prediction" for denoising, dereverberation, packet-loss concealment, target-speaker extraction, echo cancellation and separation. UNIVERSE/UNIVERSE++ use score diffusion and also cover codec artifacts and packet loss. — [arXiv 2604.14606](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.14606)
- [Paper] Per UniPASE: "purely generative models often struggle to top benchmark evaluations". In the ICASSP 2024 Speech Signal Improvement Challenge "the top-ranked systems ... all adopt a two-stage 'restoration-enhancement' framework, optimized with a combination of predictive and adversarial objectives". — [arXiv 2604.14606](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.14606)
- [Challenge] Interspeech 2025 URGENT received 32 submissions; "the best system uses a discriminative model, while most other competitive ones are hybrid". It was a sub-band RNN with about 102M parameters. — [Saijo et al., arXiv 2505.23212](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23212)
- [Challenge] At ICASSP 2026 URGENT, "leading systems dominantly followed a hybrid generative and discriminative paradigm", using generative models "for robust speech restoration and discriminative networks for precise signal preservation". Top teams stressed curated data, e.g. MOS-based filtering and pre-trained restoration models used for data cleaning. — [Li et al., arXiv 2601.13531](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.13531)
- [Paper] A 2026 controlled comparison (Fraunhofer IIS / AudioLabs Erlangen) found that generative methods help "particularly in low-SNR scenarios and mismatched conditions, [but] the complexity–performance trade-off does not always justify the performance gains, especially for diffusion-based methods". SGMSE+ (30 steps) and StoRM (50 steps) cost "at least 60 − 100× more" GMACs than GAN baselines. The authors hypothesize generative methods pay off most for "inherently generative" tasks such as bandwidth extension. — [Shetu, Habets, Brendel, arXiv 2606.02913](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02913)
- [Paper] The Diffusion Buffer brought diffusion enhancement online: an input-output latency of 0.3–1 s on a GPU (Interspeech 2025) [arXiv 2506.02908](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.02908), reduced to 32–176 ms algorithmic latency with one network call per frame (Oct 2025 extension), which "also outperforms its predictive counterpart on unseen noisy speech data". — [Lay et al., arXiv 2510.18744](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.18744)

**Why the output can sound "studio-like" (source statements)**
- [Paper] The Miipher authors state that parametric-resynthesis and diffusion methods "can convert lecture and historical speech into high-quality speech as if these were recorded in a studio". — [arXiv 2303.01664](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.01664)
- [Challenge] A DNS 2021 analysis showed that "the best theoretical Overall MOS that can be achieved is 3.92 assuming Background Noise MOS of 5 and Speech MOS same as that of noisy speech". In other words, perfect noise removal alone caps perceived quality unless the speech signal itself is improved. — [arXiv 2101.01902](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.01902)

**Product link (Adobe), stated carefully**
- [Official doc] An Adobe Research feature dated Oct 13, 2023 credits Research Scientist Jiaqi Su and Senior Research Scientist Zeyu Jin (the HiFi-GAN/HiFi-GAN-2 authors) for bringing Enhance Speech in Adobe Podcast "from early-stage research to millions of users". It does not name the model or algorithm. — [Adobe Research, "Behind the Tech"](https://research.adobe.com/news/behind-the-tech-enhance-speech-in-adobe-podcast)

### Inferences
- Masking vs generation is the core distinction. Masking subtracts and attenuates, so the voice can only get "cleaner" while keeping the microphone's coloration and bandwidth. Generation re-renders speech from a prior learned on studio corpora (DAPS, VCTK, LibriTTS-type data), so the output inherits studio-like bandwidth, EQ and room. That explains how a phone recording can come out sounding like a studio mic, and also why output can sound generic or "AI-polished". The timbre and room come from the training prior, not the original recording.
- The same mechanism creates the extra risks. When the input is ambiguous (low SNR, codec gaps, packet loss), the model fills in plausible speech, which can mean wrong phonemes or words, a voice drifting toward training-set speakers, or an accent or language drifting toward the dominant training language (see Q4). For journalism, legal or archival use, the output is a reconstruction, not a measurement. That editorial concern is my inference; the mechanism is documented.
- The field's trajectory is toward hybrids: a predictive stage to anchor content, then generative refinement (StoRM, two-stage ICASSP 2024 SSI winners, URGENT 2025/2026 leaders, UniPASE's phonetic anchoring). This is the documented answer to hallucination.
- The Adobe Research page links Enhance Speech to the HiFi-GAN authors, which makes it plausible that the product descends from that research line. Adobe does not publicly document the algorithm, so it should not be stated as fact.

### Gaps
- No arXiv version of HiFi-GAN-2 was found, and its training data was not extracted.
- Resemble Enhance has no accompanying paper or documented evaluation or limitations.
- A search-engine summary claimed Miipher-2's code and checkpoints were withheld over misuse concerns. This could not be verified in the paper text or on the demo page, so treat it as unverified.
- No primary technical documentation was found for commercial "studio" features (Adobe Enhance Speech v1/v2, NVIDIA Studio Voice, etc.).

## Q4. Documented limitations and failure modes (over-suppression, artifacts, "robotic" timbre, hallucinated phonemes/words, identity/accent/emotion change, music, laughter, overlaps, singing, non-English incl. Korean, very low SNR, clipping)

### Takeaway
Predictive enhancers fail mainly by distorting or suppressing speech: over-suppression, dulled high frequencies, "dry" or "robotic" sound, and muting the main talker when another voice is present. Generative enhancers fail mainly by inventing things: vocalizing or breathing sounds, substituted phonemes or words, speaker-similarity loss, and, at low SNR or on unseen languages, outputs that drift toward the dominant training language. Very low SNR, overlapping speakers, strong reverberation, real (non-simulated) recordings, atypical speech (whisper, singing, extreme emotion) and unseen languages are the documented hard cases. Korean appears only as an unseen test language in URGENT 2026, with no per-language results published in the overview.

### Cited Findings
**Over-suppression and speech distortion (mostly predictive models)**
- [Challenge] Interspeech 2021 DNS: the top models reached a background-noise DMOS of about 2 (MOS 4.6), but the best overall MOS was about 3.78, "about 1.2 DMOS less than the perfect quality ... achieving superior noise suppression without distorting speech is a challenging problem." Organizers had also noted that "even the best noise suppressor was far from achieving superior speech quality in challenging scenarios". — [arXiv 2101.01902](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.01902)
- [Challenge] ICASSP 2023 DNS: "From prior editions, we learnt that improving signal quality (SIG) is challenging particularly in presence of simultaneously active interfering talkers and noise". The results showed SIG degradation "for most of the models", partly because "Personalized model end up suppressing the primary talker". — [arXiv 2303.11510](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11510)
- [Challenge] "the model performance degrades significantly on real recordings" compared with synthetic test sets. — [arXiv 2005.13981](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.13981)
- [Paper] Iwamoto et al. (NTT) decomposed enhancement errors into noise and artifact components: "We experimentally identify the artifact component as the main cause of performance degradation" for ASR. The remedy, "observation adding" (mixing some of the noisy input back in), "improves ASR performance for both simulated and real recordings". — [arXiv 2201.06685](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.06685)
- [Paper] Demucs found that "a small amount of bleeding (5%) improves the overall perceived quality". — [arXiv 2006.12847](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.12847)
- [Paper] A metric-optimized model (MetricGAN+) concentrated energy "in the low- and mid-frequency area ... while high frequencies are strongly attenuated". — [arXiv 2208.05830](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05830)

**"Robotic", rough or overly dry timbre**
- [Paper] "not enough [comb] filtering results in roughness, whereas too much results in a robotic voice". — [arXiv 2008.04259](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.04259)
- [Paper] RNNoise output "can sometimes sound overly dry, lacking the minimum expected level of reverberation". — [arXiv 1709.08243](https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.08243)

**Hallucinated phonemes or words (generative models)**
- [Paper] Diffusion SE produces "'vocalizing' artifacts with very poor articulation and no linguistic meaning" at very low SNR, plus "breathing sounds or speech-like sounds ... in noisy regions where no speech was originally present". — [arXiv 2208.05830](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05830)
- [Paper] The pure diffusion model "is prone to confuse phonemes and generate vocalizing artifacts when facing very adverse conditions ... this naturally leads to a degradation, e.g. in automatic speech recognition". — [arXiv 2212.11851](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.11851)
- [Paper] Diffusion artifacts include "phoneme insertions/substitutions, hiss, breathing artifacts, and high-frequency distortions, which degrade automatic speech recognition (ASR) performance despite favorable perceptual scores". Outputs are "inherently stochastic", varying across runs. A semantic-consistency ensemble over several diffusion runs cut WER by 15% in low-SNR conditions. — [Chhaglani et al. (Meta), ArtiFree, arXiv 2509.19495](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.19495)
- [Challenge] URGENT 2025 defined hallucination as "discrepancies in spoken content or speaker characteristics between the noisy speech and enhanced speech". For the purely generative entry, "the model occasionally hallucinated spoken content, particularly under low-SNR conditions", and similar effects appeared "in inpainted frames affected by packet loss". — [arXiv 2505.23212](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23212)
- [Paper] In the 2026 comparison, hallucination was limited at moderate SNR, but "in very low SNR scenarios (e.g., below −7 dB), these metrics degrade significantly ... we also observed spurious spectral content in the enhanced spectrograms of the generative methods which was not present in the original clean speech." — [arXiv 2606.02913](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02913)
- [Paper] FINALLY: on severely degraded input "some artefacts remain", attributed to "the high uncertainty of the initial signal, such as the speaker's voice". — [arXiv 2410.05920](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.05920)

**Speaker identity, accent/language and emotion changes**
- [Paper] The leading purely generative URGENT 2025 system "achieved the highest perceptual scores but suffered from substantial declines in speaker similarity and character accuracy". — [arXiv 2604.14606](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.14606)
- [Paper] Miipher adds a speaker embedding "since SSL features often lose speaker information". — [arXiv 2303.01664](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.01664)
- [Challenge] Language dependency: in URGENT 2025, "the discriminative models (T1 and T10) appear to be relatively insensitive to language variations". The purely generative latent-diffusion-plus-vocoder model "demonstrates a high degree of language dependency, with its performance markedly declining when applied to an unseen language, namely Japanese". "For Japanese inputs ... the output under low-SNR conditions sometimes resembled English or other European languages that dominated the training data." Training and validation languages were EN, DE, FR, ES and ZH, with Japanese unseen. — [arXiv 2505.23212](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23212)
- [Official doc] NVIDIA's denoiser documentation says it "retains emotive tones in speech, such as happy, sad, excited, and angry tones", which were "removed as noise in previous releases of the SDK". It adds that "Extreme emotive cases such as loud laughing, shrieking, screaming, and crying might not be retained", and that the effect "might miss some noises in the first 1-2 seconds of input audio". — [NVIDIA Audio Effects SDK docs](https://docs.nvidia.com/deeplearning/maxine/audio-effects-sdk/)
- [Challenge] URGENT 2026 deliberately added "various emotions, ages, accents, singing voices, whispering speech, and more languages", and added emotion-similarity and language-identification-accuracy metrics alongside speaker similarity and character accuracy. — [arXiv 2601.13531](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.13531)

**Music, singing, laughter, whisper and other non-standard vocal sounds**
- [Challenge] DNS 2021 noted the earlier dataset lacked "crying, yelling, laughter or singing", and added 8.8 h of singing (VocalSet) and emotional speech (CREMA-D) to the clean set so these are treated as speech. — [arXiv 2101.01902](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.01902)
- [Challenge] URGENT 2026 training noise includes the Free Music Archive (music treated as noise to remove). The speech side includes VocalSet (singing), ESD (emotional), EARS, SeniorTalk (elderly) and NNCES (children). — [arXiv 2601.13531](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.13531)
- [Paper] "DNSMOS is unreliable for atypical speech, such as whispers or speech with extreme pitch". — [arXiv 2604.14606](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.14606)
- [Paper] VoiceFixer's future work is "to restore general audio signals", i.e. the published model targets speech. — [arXiv 2109.13731](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.13731)

**Overlapping speakers**
- [Challenge] "Overlapped speech, strong wideband / instantaneous noise, and high reverberation remain the hardest challenges". — [arXiv 2506.01611](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.01611)
- [Challenge] Improving SIG is especially hard "in presence of simultaneously active interfering talkers". — [arXiv 2303.11510](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11510)

**Non-English languages and Korean**
- [Paper] Some early systems were trained multilingually. RNNoise used the 21-language NTT database [arXiv 1709.08243](https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.08243); PercepNet used more than 20 languages [arXiv 2008.04259](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.04259); DeepFilterNet3 used the multilingual DNS4 data [arXiv 2305.08227](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.08227).
- [Challenge] Korean: the ICASSP 2026 URGENT blind test "includes five unseen languages (HI/KR/AR/JP/IT) beyond those used in the training data". The overview paper does not report per-language scores. — [arXiv 2601.13531](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.13531)
- [Challenge] URGENT 2025's P.808 subjective test was run "only on English data", and "generative models are expected not to hallucinate the content heavily on English data as the majority of the training data was English". A follow-up localized P.808 testing to non-English languages. — [arXiv 2505.23212](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23212); [Sach et al., arXiv 2507.11306](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.11306)
- [Paper] Sidon evaluated 100 of FLEURS's 102 languages (excluding Filipino and Oriya for lack of ASR models). No per-language Korean result was extracted. — [arXiv 2509.17052](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.17052)

**Very low SNR, clipping, codecs, bandwidth, packet loss**
- [Paper] DeepFilterNet returns a silent spectrum when estimated local SNR is below −10 dB (design rule). — [arXiv 2305.08227](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.08227)
- [Paper] At 0 dB SNR, Demucs-enhanced LibriSpeech dev-clean still had 6.9% WER vs 2.1% for clean audio (12.0% noisy). — [arXiv 2006.12847](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.12847)
- [Paper] Missing phonemes caused by codecs or downsampling are "an unrecoverable error" without text conditioning. — [arXiv 2303.01664](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.01664)
- [Challenge] Universal-enhancement benchmarks now include clipping, bandwidth limitation, codec loss (MP3/OGG), packet loss and wind noise as well as noise and reverb, with up to five distortions per input. — [arXiv 2505.23212](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23212); [arXiv 2601.13531](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.13531)
- [Paper] VoiceFixer's general model was slightly worse than a dedicated declipping model on declipping. — [arXiv 2109.13731](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.13731)

### Inferences
- The two families fail in opposite directions. Predictive models err toward losing information (muffled, dry, robotic, or the wanted talker suppressed). Generative models err toward adding information (invented syllables, breaths, a "corrected" voice or accent). For editorial use, generative errors are more dangerous because they sound natural and pass non-intrusive quality metrics (see Q5).
- Korean-specific risk is undocumented. The URGENT 2025 evidence (generative models degrading on unseen Japanese and drifting toward European languages) suggests Korean speech under heavy noise could be at similar risk with generative restorers trained mostly on English or European data. That is an extrapolation, not a measured result. Discriminative models appeared largely language-insensitive in the same study.
- Laughter, sighs, crying and singing are legitimately ambiguous to a speech-vs-noise model. Both the DNS data changes and NVIDIA's documented history (emotive tones once "removed as noise") indicate this was a real failure mode that vendors and researchers actively patched.

### Gaps
- No dedicated peer-reviewed study was found on whether enhancement removes laughter or other non-verbal vocalizations. Evidence is indirect: dataset additions and the NVIDIA documentation.
- No Korean-specific speech-enhancement evaluation with published results was found. URGENT 2026 includes Korean in the blind test, but per-language results were not in the overview paper. Individual team reports were not checked.
- The DNS Challenge per-category results for singing, emotional and non-English test clips were not found in the papers read.
- A search summary relayed a May 2026 third-party guide claiming that Adobe's FAQ says Enhance Speech results depend on how audible the speaker is relative to noise, and that the tool can "flatten ambience, mis-handle overlapping voices, or create an artificial tone" on difficult sources. Neither the guide nor Adobe's FAQ was retrieved, so this is unverified and excluded from the findings.

## Q5. How enhancement quality is measured (PESQ, STOI, SI-SDR, DNSMOS, NISQA, MOS / MUSHRA) and those metrics' caveats

### Takeaway
There are four families. Intrusive signal-fidelity metrics need the clean reference: PESQ (ITU-T P.862/P.862.2), STOI/ESTOI, SI-SDR. Non-intrusive learned MOS predictors do not: DNSMOS, NISQA, UTMOS, SCOREQ. Human listening tests are crowdsourced ITU-T P.808 ACR, P.835 SIG/BAK/OVRL, and lab MUSHRA. Newer content and identity checks are also used: WER/CER, Levenshtein phoneme similarity, SpeechBERTScore and speaker similarity. Each family has documented blind spots. PESQ can be gamed and misjudges perceptually helpful processing. Intrusive metrics penalize generative output. Non-intrusive metrics and even P.808 listening tests reward natural-sounding hallucinations. Best practice since 2024 is to combine metric categories and always include a content-fidelity metric.

### Cited Findings
**Intrusive metrics (need a clean reference)**
- [Paper] PESQ is ITU-T P.862, defined as "an objective method for end-to-end speech quality assessment of narrow-band telephone networks and speech codecs" (reference title). RNNoise had to resample its 48 kHz output to 16 kHz "due to the limitations of wideband PESQ". — [arXiv 1709.08243](https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.08243). The wideband P.862.2 version ranges "from 0.5 to 4.5". — [arXiv 2006.12847](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.12847)
- [Paper] PercepNet: "subjective testing clearly shows the limitations of PESQ-WB when evaluating the envelope postfilter – even though the subjective evaluation shows a strong improvement from the postfilter, PESQ-WB considers it a degradation." — [arXiv 2008.04259](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.04259)
- [Paper] PESQ can be gamed. MetricGAN+ "performed best among all baselines for PESQ" but got a median MUSHRA score below 50. "We suspect that MetricGAN+ has simply learned to utilize the internal operations of the PESQ algorithm to obtain a high value in this metric, neglecting the naturalness". — [arXiv 2208.05830](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05830)
- [Paper] SI-SDR: the original BSS_eval SDR "has generally been improperly used and abused"; papers claimed "differences on the order of 0.1 dB" as proof of superiority. Scale-invariant SDR is "a simpler, more robust measure". — [Le Roux et al., arXiv 1811.02508](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.02508)
- [Paper] PESQ and STOI "fail to capture phoneme-level errors". Intrusive metrics capture "energy-based distortions at the signal-level, while non-intrusive predictors favor naturalness and overrate generative outputs". — [arXiv 2509.19495](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.19495)
- [Paper] Comparing the same backbone trained predictively vs generatively: "the generative model is often favored by non-intrusive metrics of speech quality/naturalness, while being penalized to a greater extent by intrusive metrics, in particular at low input SNRs." — [de Oliveira et al., arXiv 2306.03014](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03014)
- [Paper] Generative "'vocalizing' artifacts ... or phonetic confusions may not be captured" by standard metrics. — [arXiv 2208.05830](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05830)

**Non-intrusive learned predictors**
- [Paper] DNSMOS (Reddy, Gopal, Cutler, who also organized the DNS Challenge) is a reference-free MOS predictor built for noise suppressors, because "conventional and widely used metrics require a reference clean speech signal, which is unavailable in real recordings" and older no-reference methods "correlate poorly with human ratings". — [Reddy, Gopal, Cutler, arXiv 2010.15258](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.15258)
- [Paper] DNSMOS P.835 predicts SIG (speech), BAK (background) and OVRL (overall), with Pearson correlation "PCC=0.94 for SIG and PCC=0.98 for BAK and OVRL" (per-model level; clip-level also reported). — [arXiv 2110.01763](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.01763)
- [Paper] NISQA (Mittag, Naderi, Chehadi, Möller; Interspeech 2021) predicts overall quality plus "Noisiness, Coloration, Discontinuity, and Loudness". It was trained and evaluated on 81 datasets and focuses on communication-network distortions. — [Mittag et al., arXiv 2104.09494](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.09494)
- [Challenge] "DNSMOS yields high scores even when hallucinations occur ... the other two non-intrusive metrics exhibited similar trends", so non-intrusive metrics should be combined "with other metrics that can penalize hallucination, such as CAcc". — [arXiv 2505.23212](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23212)
- [Challenge] URGENT 2024 analysis: newer non-intrusive metrics "such as UTMOS and VQScore show better perceptual correlations than traditional ones (e.g., DNSMOS)"; UTMOS and SCOREQ ranked highest among objective metrics. WADA-SNR is "unsuitable for measuring enhanced audio quality". "Using solely a single category of metrics, especially non-intrusive metrics ... can be unreliable or even misleading." The overall multi-metric ranking reached a Kendall correlation of 0.73 with MOS rank. — [arXiv 2506.01611](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.01611)
- [Paper] "DNSMOS is unreliable for atypical speech, such as whispers or speech with extreme pitch". — [arXiv 2604.14606](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.14606)

**Human listening tests**
- [Challenge] DNS 2020: conventional metrics (PESQ, POLQA, ViSQOL, SDR) "are shown to not correlate well with subjective tests", and "lab subjective tests are not scalable for a large test set". The organizers therefore open-sourced a crowdsourced ITU-T P.808 framework (ACR). — [arXiv 2005.13981](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.13981)
- [Challenge] From 2021 the DNS Challenge used ITU-T P.835, which "gives additional information about the quality of processed speech and residual noise" (separate SIG, BAK and OVRL scores). — [arXiv 2101.01902](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.01902)
- [Paper] MUSHRA (multi-stimulus with hidden reference) tests were used by SGMSE+ (10 participants, 12 examples) and StoRM (webMUSHRA). StoRM found listeners "put more weight on 'residual distortions' and 'potential artifacts' (vocalizing/breathing/confusions) than on 'speech distortion'". — [arXiv 2208.05830](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05830); [arXiv 2212.11851](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.11851)
- [Challenge] P.808 is reference-free. Listeners answered "How do you rate the overall quality of the following speech sample?", so "it would be difficult to penalize the correctness of the spoken content and speaker consistency as long as the speech sounds natural". In URGENT 2025 the purely generative entry had the highest MOS (3.69) but ranked 13th overall. — [arXiv 2505.23212](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23212); see also [arXiv 2604.14606](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.14606)
- [Paper] For generative SE, "subjective ACR MOS and objective reference-free metrics (DNSMOS, NISQA)" should be "accompanied by objective phone fidelity metrics to reliably detect hallucinations". — [Sach et al., arXiv 2507.11306](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.11306)

**Content and identity metrics, and multi-metric protocols**
- [Paper] ArtiFree cites Levenshtein phoneme distance (LPD) and a "hallucination error rate (HER)" as metrics proposed to fill this gap; LPD is attributed to Pirklbauer et al. — [arXiv 2509.19495](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.19495) (Pirklbauer et al., ITG 2023 proceedings page: [VDE Verlag](https://vde-verlag.de/proceedings-en/456164052.html); its content was seen only via a search-result summary)
- [Paper] FINALLY computes phoneme error rate (PhER) and WER by running wav2vec2-based ASR on reference and enhanced audio. — [arXiv 2410.05920](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.05920)
- [Challenge] URGENT 2025 used 14 metrics: non-intrusive (DNSMOS, NISQA, UTMOS); intrusive (POLQA, PESQ, ESTOI, SDR, MCD, LSD); task-independent (Levenshtein phoneme similarity, SpeechBERTScore); task-dependent (speaker similarity, character accuracy); plus P.808 MOS. Ranking averages ranks per category, "inspired by the Friedman test". — [arXiv 2505.23212](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23212)
- [Challenge] ICASSP 2026 URGENT added SCOREQ, emotion similarity and language-ID accuracy, made final rankings with P.808 ACR MOS plus CCR (comparison MOS), and introduced a track for MOS prediction of SE-processed speech. — [arXiv 2601.13531](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.13531)
- [Challenge] The ICASSP 2023 DNS Challenge's score combined subjective quality and ASR word accuracy: Final score = 0.5[WAcc + 0.25(OVRL − 1)]. — [arXiv 2303.11510](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11510)

### Inferences
- STOI barely separated successive DeepFilterNet versions (0.942 → 0.944) while PESQ moved 2.81 → 3.17 [arXiv 2305.08227](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.08227). STOI appears to be near its ceiling on the standard VoiceBank+DEMAND benchmark, so it is weak for ranking modern systems there. This is an inference from the published table.
- No single number can certify a "polished" recording. A trustworthy evaluation for voice polishing needs at least: (a) a perceptual score (MOS or a strong non-intrusive predictor such as UTMOS/SCOREQ), (b) a content-fidelity score (WER/CER or phoneme similarity against the original transcript), and (c) speaker similarity. This mirrors the URGENT protocol.
- Leaderboard claims based only on DNSMOS/NISQA, or only on PESQ, should be treated skeptically: the first rewards hallucination, the second can be gamed and penalizes generative output.

### Gaps
- The standard documents themselves (ITU-T P.862/P.863/P.808/P.835, ITU-R BS.1534 MUSHRA, the Taal et al. STOI paper) were not fetched. Their scope statements are cited only indirectly.
- No source found quantifying how well DNSMOS or NISQA track human judgments on Korean speech.

## Q6. Real-time vs offline enhancement: latency budgets and model design (Krisp, NVIDIA RTX Voice/Broadcast, Apple Voice Isolation vs offline file enhancement)

### Takeaway
Real-time call/stream suppressors are causal frame-by-frame models. They have a total algorithmic latency of about 40 ms or less (DNS Challenge rule), use roughly 10–20 ms frames with little look-ahead, and must cost a few percent of a CPU core: RNNoise, PercepNet, DeepFilterNet and causal Demucs fit this envelope. Hearables push to sub-millisecond algorithmic latency. Offline file enhancement can use bidirectional context, large self-supervised encoders, many diffusion steps and big vocoders. Generative restorers are typically offline: FINALLY explicitly "doesn't suit low latency scenarios", and SGMSE+ runs slower than real time even on a GPU. Since 2025, diffusion has been pushed online (Diffusion Buffer at 32–176 ms on a GPU). Vendors publish only high-level facts: Krisp claims about 15–20 ms algorithmic latency on-device; NVIDIA documents 10/20 ms frames and Tensor-Core GPUs; Apple says only that Voice Isolation "uses machine learning". Model architectures are not publicly documented.

### Cited Findings
**Research latency budgets**
- [Challenge] DNS 2020 real-time track: an algorithm "must take less than T/2 (in ms) to process a frame of size T (in ms) on an Intel Core i5 quad-core machine clocked at 2.4 GHz", with T ≤ 40 ms, and may use "only 40ms of future frames". A non-real-time track had no compute limit. — [arXiv 2005.13981](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.13981)
- [Challenge] DNS 2021 tightened the rule: "total algorithmic latency allowed including the frame size T, stride time Ts, and any look ahead must be ≤ 40ms". A 20 ms frame with a 10 ms stride (30 ms) passes; a 32 ms frame with a 16 ms stride (48 ms) fails. — [arXiv 2101.01902](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.01902)
- [Paper] Measured designs:
  - RNNoise: 20 ms window, 10 ms hop, about 1.3% of a laptop core [arXiv 1709.08243](https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.08243)
  - PercepNet: 10 ms frames, 40 ms look-ahead, 4.1% of a core [arXiv 2008.04259](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.04259)
  - DeepFilterNet3: 40 ms overall latency, real-time factor 0.19 single-threaded [arXiv 2305.08227](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.08227)
  - Causal Demucs: 40 ms frame including 3 ms look-ahead, 16 ms stride, real-time factor 0.6–1.05 on a quad-core i5, 0.8 on a single core; the authors call it "realistic to use ... along a video call software" [arXiv 2006.12847](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.12847)
- [Paper] Hearables extreme: Google's minimum-phase FIR approach (FIR taps generated by a 626k-parameter LSTM) reaches "mean algorithmic latency of 0.32 ms to 1.25 ms" and "a mean end-to-end latency of 3.35 ms" on a low-power DSP, with only 4.1 dB SI-SDR improvement, illustrating the latency–quality trade-off. — [Dementyev et al., arXiv 2409.18239](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.18239)
- [Paper] Conv-TasNet's time-domain design was motivated partly by "the long latency in calculating the spectrograms". — [arXiv 1809.07454](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.07454)

**Offline / generative cost**
- [Paper] SGMSE+ real-time factor is 0.89–2.65 on an RTX 2080 Ti depending on sampler steps (30–90 network evaluations). — [arXiv 2208.05830](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05830)
- [Paper] StoRM cuts diffusion compute by about 10×. — [arXiv 2212.11851](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.11851)
- [Paper] Diffusion SE "inference requires multiple reverse diffusion steps, leading to high latency unsuitable for real-time deployment"; adaptive step counts trade artifact suppression against latency. — [arXiv 2509.19495](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.19495)
- [Paper] FINALLY has a real-time factor of 0.03 on a V100 GPU (fast throughput) but "doesn't suit low latency scenarios ... The big obstacle ... could be the WavLM model that utilizes context". — [arXiv 2410.05920](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.05920)
- [Paper] Batch-throughput restorers for dataset cleaning: Miipher-2 has a real-time factor of 0.0078 on a TPU [arXiv 2505.04457](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.04457); Sidon runs "up to 500 × faster than real time on a single GPU" [arXiv 2509.17052](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.17052). These are throughput figures, not streaming latency.
- [Paper] Online generative SE: the Diffusion Buffer reached 0.3–1 s input-output latency on a GPU [arXiv 2506.02908](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.02908), then 32–176 ms algorithmic latency with one network call per frame on a consumer GPU [arXiv 2510.18744](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.18744).

### Inferences
- The latency budget dictates the model family. Within about 40 ms you can afford causal masking or filtering models with small recurrent state, and generative regeneration of missing content is mostly off the table. Offline you can afford whole-utterance context (bidirectional networks, WavLM/USM encoders, many diffusion steps), which is where "studio-like" restoration lives. A rough rule follows: live call tools mostly suppress, file-based "enhance speech" tools increasingly regenerate. This is my inference; product internals are undocumented.
- The 2025 Diffusion Buffer results show the boundary is moving, but they still need a GPU and 32–176 ms, more than typical call-suppression budgets.
- Vendor latency numbers (Krisp 15–20 ms) are "algorithmic latency" claims. End-to-end device latency (buffers, OS audio stack) will be higher and is not documented.

### Gaps
- No public technical description was found for NVIDIA RTX Voice/Broadcast models (architecture, latency), Apple Voice Isolation (model, latency, on-device details beyond "machine learning"), or Krisp's architecture. Statements about which algorithm these products use would be unsupported.
- Whether NVIDIA Broadcast uses the same models as the Maxine AFX SDK is not stated in the sources retrieved.
- Apple's later expansion of Voice Isolation to recording apps (reported in search results as iOS 26 for Voice Memos) was not verified from Apple's support page.
