## 1. Q3 2026 capex guidance (2026 and 2027) from Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon, Meta and Oracle, and the market reaction

### Takeaway
No hyperscaler raised its 2026 capex number in late October 2026. Alphabet held $195–205B, Amazon held about $220B, and Meta narrowed to $135–145B (raising the floor). Microsoft reportedly trimmed to $170B from $175B because third-party GPU capacity slipped into the first half of 2027. All of them, however, signaled sharply higher 2027 spending, and Street and Goldman estimates put 2027 hyperscaler capex at roughly $0.93–1.2 trillion. Stocks fell where free cash flow (FCF) turned negative: Alphabet −10%, Meta about −7%, Amazon −6% after hours. Only Microsoft rose (+4% after hours), and it was the one still FCF-positive.

### Inferences
- Adding up the midpoints (Alphabet about $200B, Amazon $220B, Microsoft about $170B, Meta $140B) gives roughly **$730B for 2026**; Microsoft's period basis is unclear. A 2027 total of $0.93–1.2T would mean another 27–64% of growth on a base that already exceeds operating cash flow.
- The Q3 "pause" in guidance looks like a supply bottleneck (GPU-as-a-service delays, memory costs), not weak demand; management says demand still exceeds supply. That differs from 2001, when carriers cut capex because demand never arrived. The flip side is that the spending is pushed into 2027, not cancelled.
- The market punished **negative FCF, not capex itself**. FCF-positive Microsoft rose, while FCF-negative Alphabet, Meta and Amazon fell 6–10%. Investors now treat funding as the swing factor, which is the "2000-like" channel in the Trefis framing.

### Gaps
- Whether Microsoft's $170B (cut from $175B) is a fiscal-2027 or calendar-year figure. The two CNBC articles read differently; check the 8-K or call transcript.
- Meta's 2027 qualitative capex and expense commentary from the call transcript (not retrieved).
- Oracle's fiscal Q2 2027 results (due around December) are not out, so the September guidance is the latest.
- Alphabet's 2027 number exists only as CNBC's ">$300B" characterization; there is no company figure.

## 2. How the build-out is funded (cash vs bonds, equity, leases, private credit, vendor/circular financing); major 2026 bond deals; credit spreads of AI-linked issuers

### Takeaway
The build-out has moved from cash-funded to externally funded. Three of the big four (all but Microsoft) had negative FCF in Q3 2026. Hyperscaler bond issuance reached roughly $220–300B in 2026, against about $108B in all of 2025 for the four largest issuers. Meta launched a $95B equity program on top of a $20–25B bond sale. Credit is splitting into three tiers: AA/A hyperscalers whose CDS have widened sharply from low levels, Oracle (BBB-, "trading like high yield"), and CoreWeave (CDS around 1,100–1,200bp, yields around 15%, i.e. distressed). Vendor and circular financing (Nvidia backstops and stakes in OpenAI, CoreWeave and Meta) keeps growing.

### Inferences
- Funding now runs through **four channels at once**: IG bonds (Meta, Alphabet, Amazon, Oracle), new equity (Meta's $95B; Oracle's ATM and $10B share filing), off-balance-sheet leases (Oracle's $288B not yet commenced), and vendor support (Nvidia stakes and backstops in OpenAI, CoreWeave and Meta). ## 4. Democratic and state proposals on data centers, power prices and AI regulation around the midterms; realism and speed of capex impact

### Takeaway
The "blue wave" adds mainly **state and local** risk. Democratic governors-elect in Texas, Georgia, Ohio and Wisconsin promised moratoriums, pauses or full cost-pass-through to data centers. Local voters rejected data centers in every ballot measure with results but one, of 41. The five flipped governorships cover states with about 37% of planned US capacity, and Texas alone about 22.8%. Federally, Sanders and Ocasio-Cortez want a national moratorium, but analysts expect gridlock because Trump holds the veto. So the likely effect is slower permitting, interconnection and project conversion for **2028+ capacity** (UBS: about 30% of 2028 additions "at risk"), not an immediate cut to 2026–27 hyperscaler capex, which is mostly chips and already contracted.
