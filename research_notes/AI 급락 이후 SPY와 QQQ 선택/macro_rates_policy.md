## Q1. Fed policy: current target range, what the Fed did in 2026 and why, and signals for Dec 9 and 2027

### Takeaway
The fed funds target is **3.75–4.00%**. The Fed got there with a unanimous 25bp hike on **Sept 16, 2026**, its first hike in over three years. It then held on **Oct 28** by a 10–2 vote, with Cook and Hammack dissenting in favor of another hike. The September dot plot's median shows one more hike in 2026 and a flat 2027 (end-2027 median 4.1%). Hawks (Logan, Musalem, Barkin) are openly pushing for more. Futures priced about 85% odds of a Dec 9 hike on Oct 28. That fell to about 67–69% after the Nov 5 AI-led equity rout.

### Inferences
- The Fed has restarted tightening because an energy shock re-accelerated inflation while growth stayed resilient. The hawkish bloc (Logan, Musalem, Barkin, Cook, Hammack) suggests the bar for skipping December is a clear growth or market shock. The Nov 5 rout lowered the odds but has not, as of Nov 5–6 pricing, displaced a hike as the modal outcome.
- As of Oct 28, futures (about 4.7% by Sept 2027) were roughly 50–60bp more hawkish than the SEP median (4.1% end-2027). How that gap closes is a key 6–12-month driver for long-duration equities. The Nov 5 rally in Treasuries suggests futures moved toward the SEP. That is an inference; I have no post-Nov 5 2027 pricing.
- Real 10-year yields near 2.6–3.0% are at 2007–08 levels. That is a much higher discount-rate starting point than in 2022, when real yields started at −1%.
- Warsh gives no dots and little guidance. That raises sensitivity to each data release: October CPI on Nov 10, October PCE on Nov 25, and the December SEP.

## Q4. The Iran war: status, effect on oil supply and prices, and main oil scenarios

### Takeaway
The war began **Feb 28, 2026** with US/Israeli strikes. Iran closed Hormuz. The path since then:
1. Brent spot more than doubled, from $71 to **$138 by Apr 7**.
2. A June deal (the Islamabad Memorandum) briefly took Brent spot back to about **$69 (Jul 1)**.
3. The deal collapsed on **Jul 8** and Brent spot rose back to about **$135 (Oct 2)**.
4. Prices are now falling on peace hopes after the midterms: Brent futures dropped 8.6% to $94.66 on Nov 4.

Gulf exports run at about three-quarters of pre-war levels. EIA's October baseline is about $105 Brent in 4Q26 and about $84 in 2027. The range of outcomes is very wide: roughly $70 if there is a deal, above $120–140 if fighting escalates.

### Cited Findings
**Timeline** ([Wikipedia: 2026 Iran war ceasefire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Iran_war_ceasefire), cross-checked against FRED Brent):
- 28 Feb: US and Israel strike Iran and kill the supreme leader. Iran retaliates and closes Hormuz.
- 7–8 Apr: a two-week, Pakistan-mediated ceasefire. Iran limits transits and charges tolls.
- 11–12 Apr: the Islamabad talks fail.
- 13 Apr: US naval blockade of Iranian ports begins.
- 21 Apr: ceasefire extended, blockade kept.
- 11–17 Jun: 60-day ceasefire extension, ending with the Islamabad Memorandum signed Jun 17.
- **8 Jul: ceasefire collapses**, and Trump says it is "over." The blockade was reinstated on Jul 14, per [Wikipedia: 2026 US naval blockade of Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_United_States_naval_blockade_of_Iran) (via search summary).

Their later paper, NBER w34814 "Interest Rates and Equity Valuations," reportedly finds the raw link between market returns and yield changes small and imprecise until rate moves are split by cause: rate moves tied to lower expected growth are insignificant, while others show a positive relation (per abstract summary) — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/p/nbr/nberwo/34814.html)
- **GMO, "The Duration of Value and Growth"** (Mar 2021), a counterpoint: value and growth have similar effective durations once turnover is considered, so the "growth is long-duration" story is overstated — [GMO](https://gmo.com/europe/research-library/the-duration-of-value-and-growth) (via search summary)

### Inferences
- **Hikes are not the problem; the starting point is.** QQQ's worst relative episodes share three features: very high starting valuations, a sharp rise in *real* yields from very low levels, and a broken earnings narrative (2000–01, 2022). Episodes driven by strong earnings growth saw QQQ outperform even as yields rose (1994, 1996, 2013, 2017–18, 2023, early 2026). The Nasdaq study's EPS-growth explanation fits that pattern. But the study was published in Nov 2021 and failed out of sample in 2022, which is a warning against relying on the long-run average.
- **The 2004–06 analog matters in an oil shock.** That cycle combined steady hikes, Brent more than doubling and strong energy and cyclical earnings. QQQ lagged SPY by about 10pp. If Brent stays at $90–110 and the Fed keeps hiking (scenario b in Q4), the S&P 500's energy, financials and value sectors are historically favored over the Nasdaq-100.
- **Escalation-type oil spikes have hurt NDX more** (1990 −11pp, 2022 −3.4pp). In 2007–08, QQQ beat SPY because the shock coincided with a financial-sector bust concentrated in the S&P. Through 2026's two oil spikes, QQQ roughly matched or beat SPY because AI earnings dominated.
- **The post-2020 sign flip** (QQQ's excess return now falls when yields and oil rise) supports treating QQQ as a "long-duration, short-oil" position relative to SPY. But the correlations (|r| ≤ 0.28) imply rates and oil explain under 10% of monthly relative-return variance. Earnings and the AI narrative dominate, as Oct 5–Nov 5, 2026 showed: QQQ lagged about 9pp while 10-year yields fell about 45bp.
- **Real-yield level risk:** today's 10-year real yield (2.6–3.0%) is the highest since 2008. The Nasdaq study warns that hikes at high rate levels tend to hurt equities. With the Fed still hiking and real yields near cycle highs, there is less valuation cushion for QQQ than in 2015–18. This is an inference; I did not gather current P/E data.
- **After the last hike, QQQ usually outperforms** (4 of 5 cases). The one exception, 2000–01, followed a bubble and a sharp earnings collapse. Whether the AI selloff marks a 2000-type break or a 2018 Q4-type air pocket is the key distinction for 6–12-month QQQ-versus-SPY positioning. In 2018 Q4, QQQ lagged by 3.4pp and then beat SPY by 7.4pp over the following 12 months.
