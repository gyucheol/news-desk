## Q1. What are the current year-end 2026 and 2027 S&P 500 targets of major firms, and did any change after the midterms or the Nov 5 selloff?

## Q2. What do strategists recommend on tech/AI versus the broad market, and on buying this dip?

### Takeaway
Opinion is split, and timing matters. The large sell-side houses were mostly **"buy the dip / AI fundamentals intact"** before Nov 5: Morgan Stanley's Wilson, Deutsche Bank's tech upgrade, BofA's semis analyst, Citi, Goldman and HSBC. After the crash, only a few voices are on record. They range from Victoria Greene's "AI trade is not dead" to MRB's "shakeout has much further to run, diversify away from tech". There is a parallel push from BofA's Subramanian, Wells Fargo and equal-weight advocates toward **broader, equal-weight or quality exposure over cap-weighted tech**.

## Q3. What are the main bear cases, and who is making them?

### Takeaway
Four bear cases recur. (1) **AI capex, financing and earnings-quality stress**: Panmure Liberum's 5,000 call for 2027, Wells Fargo's "capex math breaks in 2028", MRB and BlackRock. (2) **Rates and liquidity under a hiking Fed**: Wilson calls liquidity "the biggest risk", the FMS's top tail risk is a disorderly rise in bond yields, and the market prices ~70% odds of a Dec 9 hike. (3) **Post-midterm policy risk to data centres**. (4) **Late-cycle valuation and sentiment red flags** (BofA). The oil shock appears mainly as an input to tightening, and oil fell sharply on Nov 4.

## Q5. How accurate have strategists' year-end targets been historically?

### Takeaway
Poor at levels and worst at turning points. Consensus year-end forecasts track direction reasonably well (correlation ~0.76 in 1999–2016) but missed hugely in downturns: they were too high by 26% on average in 2000–02 and by 64% in 2008. Consensus has essentially never forecast a down year. Targets also tend to chase price, and 2026's serial mid-year raises fit that pattern.

### Cited Findings
- Nir Kaissar (Bloomberg) studied 1999–2016. The average strategist forecast ran 5.8% above the actual year-end level, with a 0.76 correlation between forecast and outcome. Forecasts overestimated by 26.2% on average in 2000–2002 and by 64.3% in 2008. They underestimated by 10.6% in the early-2003 recovery and by 10.9% in the first half of 2009. "The forecasts were least useful when they mattered most." He blames momentum-based methods that extrapolate trends. — [Globe and Mail / Bloomberg, Dec 23, 2016](https://www.theglobeandmail.com/amp/globe-investor/investment-ideas/sp-500-forecasts-mostly-hit-mark-until-they-matter-most/article33422865/) [primary reporting]
- Bespoke: since 2000 there has been no year in which the consensus year-end target was negative. — [Bespoke via Mutual Fund Observer](https://mutualfundobserver.com/discuss/discussion/37533/bespoke-2018-wall-street-strategists-year-end-price-targets) [snippet]
- Other estimates (methods differ): forecasts were off by an average of 14.7 percentage points a year since 2000. — [Motley Fool, 2015](https://www.fool.com/investing/general/2015/02/25/the-blind-forecaster.aspx) [snippet]. Another puts the average miss at 15.7% for 2018–2023. — [Global Markets Investor](https://globalmarketsinvestor.substack.com/p/dont-be-fooled-by-wall-street-analysts) [snippet; Substack]. Another calls the 2000–2024 record "atrocious". — [Livewire](https://www.livewiremarkets.com/wires/don-t-listen-to-wall-street-if-you-want-to-know-what-markets-will-do-in-2025) [snippet]. A tracker says Stifel's Barry Bannister had a 0% hit rate within ±3% across 12 closed forecast years. — [AnaChart](https://anachart.com/?p=418561) [snippet; one site's method]
- **2026 as a live example of chasing price:** JPMorgan cut to 7,200 in March, then raised its target three times (7,600 in April, 7,800 in June, 8,000 around August). — [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/financial-accounting/jpmorgan-lifts-s-p-500-target-as-ai-rally-regains-momentum); [Sentisense](https://app.sentisense.ai/stories/jpmorgan-raises-s-p-500-price-target-amid-ai-breakout-08102026). Goldman went from 7,600 to 8,000 in May. — [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/05/27/goldman-raises-its-sp-500-year-end-forecast-its-for-one-simple-reason.html). UBS went 7,500 → 7,900 → 8,100. — [Zawya/Reuters](https://www.zawya.com/en/capital-markets/corporate-earnings/ubs-global-wealth-management-lifts-sp-500-year-end-target-to-8100-463458). Yardeni raised three times to 8,400. — [Yahoo](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/ed-yardeni-raises-p-500-125931405.html). BofA's original 7,100 (the Street low in Dec 2025) and Oppenheimer's 8,100 (the Street high) bracketed the year. — [roic](https://www.roic.ai/news/bofa-issues-most-bearish-sp-500-outlook-for-2026-forecasting-just-4-upside-amid-ai-valuation-concerns-12-15-2025); [MarketScreener](https://in.marketscreener.com/news/oppenheimer-sets-street-high-8-100-s-p-500-target-for-2026-ce7d51ddd081f127)
- Strategists tend to hold targets through selloffs. The Bloomberg survey saw only two of 19 change their targets in October despite the AI selloff. — [Yahoo UK/Bloomberg survey](https://uk.finance.yahoo.com/news/p-500-forecast-holds-7-130000607.html)

### Inferences
- The +8.3% consensus upside from the Nov 5 close should be read as stale and structurally optimistic. On history, it is most likely to be wrong exactly at a turning point, if this is one. The more useful signals are the direction of revisions in the next few weeks and the spread between bulls and bears.
