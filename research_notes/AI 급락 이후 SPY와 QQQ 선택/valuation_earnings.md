- **Yardeni's 18.2x is not an error.** The Oct 30 close of 7,580.07 was 1.8% below Oct 29.
  - Yardeni defines forward earnings as a time-weighted average of current- and next-year consensus. This is background knowledge, not re-verified this session.
  - Applied to LSEG's CY26 $366.29 and CY27 $425.24 at a 2/12 and 10/12 weighting, that gives ≈$415.4 and 18.25x at the Oct 30 close.
  - The same EPS gives 18.51x at the Nov 4 close, which matches Benzinga's 18.5x. So Benzinga's data are probably as of Nov 4, before the selloff.
- **The 18.2–19.1x spread across sources has two causes in roughly equal parts:**
  - The price date: 7,744 vs 7,580 is 2.2%.
  - The EPS definition: LSEG's sum-of-next-4-quarters $405.93 vs time-weighted $413–416 is 1.7–2.6%. F4Q excludes the high Q4'27 ($114.89) and includes Q4'26 ($94.98).
- **FactSet's implied ≈$412.9 is consistent with its own figures.** A time-weighted blend of FactSet CY26 ($363.94) and implied CY27 ($423.3) gives ≈$413.4.
- **SPY's 20.23 "FY1" P/E is not comparable** with index forward-12-month figures.
  - FY1 means each company's current fiscal year, which is mostly CY2026 EPS (calc: 7,688.62 ÷ $366.29 = 21.0x). For off-calendar firms such as Apple, NVIDIA and Microsoft, FY1 has already rolled into FY2027. The ETF figure is also aggregated across holdings.
  - So index-vs-ETF and FY1-vs-NTM, not valuation, explain why it reads higher than 18.6x.
- **On a calendar-year basis at the Nov 5 close (calc):** 20.1–20.2x CY2026 EPS and 17.3–17.4x CY2027 EPS. At about 14.9x CY2028 (LSEG $494.02), it is consistent with QuantStreet's "<16x two-year-forward".
- **Versus history (calc):** the Nov 5 multiple of about 17.8x (NTM basis) is 6.8% below FactSet's 10-yr average of 19.1 and 10.1% below the 5-yr average of 19.8.
- **Why the late-2025 22–23x → ~18x drop is real:**
  - LSEG's CY2026 growth estimate went from 15.6% on Jan 1 to 36.3% now. Calc: CY26 EPS rose from about $313.6 to $366.29, about +17%.
  - FactSet's forward EPS rose another 2.5% in October alone.
  - Yahoo's May 2026 forward yield of about 4.5% implies the index was still about 22x in May. Over the year, earnings outran price.
  - The caveat is the quality of the "E", covered in Q4 and Q7: semis/memory, the oil spike and GAAP one-offs.
- **Trailing P/E does not reconcile:** FactSet 25.9 (Oct 29), LSEG 22.8 (Oct 28), SSGA index 23.83 (Nov 4).
  - LSEG's trailing-4-quarter EPS includes the blended Q3'26 and an outsized Q2'26 ($100.32; +53.7% y/y; Comm Services +114%, Consumer Discretionary +91%). Those quarters probably include non-recurring gains (inference), which would make the LSEG trailing P/E look lower.
  - FactSet's trailing EPS (≈$298 implied) uses a different window and basis. Treat trailing P/Es as vendor-specific.

## 7. Implications: relative valuation risk of QQQ vs SPY (inference)

### Takeaway
**Inference.** On consensus numbers after Nov 5, QQQ is more attractively valued than SPY relative to history:
- Both indexes trade at about 18x forward.
- The NDX's usual 20–25% premium has collapsed to about 0–3%.
- The NDX is 20–28% below its own averages, against 7–10% for the S&P 500.

But QQQ's "E" carries more estimate risk. Its cheapness rests on AI/semiconductor earnings that the market is already pricing as cyclical (semis at 13.5x), and the selloff's trigger was capex-peak fear.

SPY's earnings support is more diversified in breadth, but not in growth: about 79% of the S&P 500's expected 2027 EPS growth comes from Tech, and the Mag 7 are about a third of SPY. Both offer a thin ERP of about +0.7–0.8pp against a 4.84% 10-yr.

### Inferences
- **Relative valuation favors QQQ** on forward P/E, discount to its own history and growth-adjusted terms. Tech's expected 2027 growth (+41.5%) is about 2.5x the S&P 500's (+16.2%) at a similar or lower multiple.
- **Sensitivity of the cushion (calc, Nov 5 prices):**

  | Forward EPS cut | NDX P/E (from ~18.0x) | S&P 500 P/E (from ~17.8x) |
  |---|---|---|
  | −10% | about 20.0x | about 19.8x (its 5-yr average) |
  | −20% | about 22.5x (still at or below its ~23x 10-yr average) | about 22.3x (well above its averages) |

  - Against its own history, QQQ can absorb a larger estimate cut before it looks expensive.
  - The cut QQQ faces is also likely larger, because its EPS is concentrated in semis and hyperscaler capex beneficiaries.
- **The risk asymmetry is about "E", not "P".** The Nov 5 drop came while estimates were still rising (Q5), so the market is discounting future cuts.
  - If NVIDIA (Nov 17) and capex guidance confirm 2027 tech growth near +40%, QQQ's compressed multiple offers more re-rating upside.
  - If 2027 tech estimates are cut, QQQ's earnings base erodes faster than SPY's.
  - A 13.5x semis multiple on +132% y/y semis growth is the classic sign of a market treating earnings as peak-cycle.
- **SPY is not an AI hedge.** About a third of SPY is the Mag 7, and Tech drives about 79% of 2027 index EPS growth. Ex-Tech, 2027 growth is about 5%.

- **Rate sensitivity is now similar.** The forward ERPs are about equal (+0.6–0.8pp), so a renewed rise in the 10-yr toward 5.3% would compress both. QQQ would probably be hit harder given its longer-duration growth profile and higher beta (judgment, not measured).
- **Net:** QQQ has higher expected return but higher estimate risk; SPY has lower valuation risk but similar thin compensation against bonds. This framing is inference from the cited data and is not a recommendation.
