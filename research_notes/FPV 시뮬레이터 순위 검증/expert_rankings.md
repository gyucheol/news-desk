## 1. How recognised reviewers rank the simulators, and for what purpose

### Takeaway
No major reviewer publishes a single overall ranking. The two most-cited experts both rank **by purpose**:
- **Oscar Liang** (guide published 29 Aug 2026; edit history "Aug 2026 – Updated recommendations"):
  - Liftoff: best for beginners
  - Uncrashed: best for freestyle
  - TRYP FPV: best for cinematic flying
  - VelociDrone: best for racing
  - Liftoff: Micro Drones: best Tiny Whoop sim
  - FPV SkyDive: best free sim
  - DRL Simulator: "DO NOT purchase" (warning dated 17 Jan 2026)
- **Joshua Bardwell's** main ranking video is from **30 Dec 2021**. Its chapter titles give: SkyDive best free, VelociDrone best racing, Uncrashed best freestyle, Tiny Whoop GO best free whoop sim, FPV Freerider best for low-spec PCs, DRL best value. Several of these picks have since been overtaken by events: DRL's server, and Tiny Whoop GO is no longer free.
- **Not ranked by any established reviewer I found:** Bodycam (its drone update came after Oscar's August 2026 revision), the Propwash Minecraft mod, RealFlight and AeroSIM RC. FPV YouTubers have made gameplay videos of Bodycam and Propwash, but these are not rankings.

### Inferences
- **The stable expert consensus, roughly 2021 to 2026:**
  - VelociDrone is the racing and physics benchmark.
  - Uncrashed and TRYP are the freestyle and visuals picks.
  - Liftoff is the all-rounder and beginner pick, with the most content and community.
  - Liftoff: Micro Drones is the whoop pick.
  - FPV SkyDive is the free starter, but its physics rate as weak.
  - DCL and FPV Freerider sit low, as casual or outdated.
  - DRL moved from "best value" (Bardwell 2021) to "do not buy" (Oscar 2026).
- **How to judge a reel's tier list against this:**
  - A list ranking Liftoff, VelociDrone, Uncrashed and TRYP in the top tiers matches the experts, as long as it reflects their different purposes.
  - A list putting DRL high in 2026, or SkyDive top for *realism*, or DCL top for *training*, contradicts the most current expert guide.
  - A single "S-tier for everything" pick is not how the experts frame it; Oscar explicitly says "you don't have to choose!".
- **Bodycam and Propwash** have no established-reviewer ranking. Any tier placement for them is the creator's own opinion. The most one can say is that FPV YouTubers have covered them (see §3).
- Bardwell's 2021 labels are about five years old. Two are clearly stale (DRL, Tiny Whoop GO's "free"). A reel citing "Bardwell's picks" may be relying on old information.

## 2. What each expert says about physics, controller/radio support, rates, maps and multiplayer, PC requirements and price (evidence by simulator)

### Takeaway
- **Experts agree on input devices.** Oscar Liang says a real RC radio over USB, which shows up as a joystick, is the right input, and DJI RC2/RC3 controllers are plug-and-play. He advises against gamepads and keyboards. No dedicated sim strictly *requires* a radio: most accept a gamepad, and several Steam pages say "only playable with a game controller or remote" (no keyboard).
- **Physics verdicts (Oscar Liang 2026):**
  - VelociDrone: "top-notch"
  - Liftoff: "slightly 'floaty'"
  - TRYP: quads "slightly heavier than in real life" but "reasonably accurate"
  - Uncrashed: "average", but rated before its Physics 3.0 update of 21 Oct 2026
  - FPV SkyDive: "mediocre"
  - DCL: "unrealistic"
  - Liftoff: Micro Drones: whoops carry "slightly more momentum than in real life"
  - Tiny Whoop GO: "slightly easier to fly than in real life"
- **Rates:** Betaflight-style rate and throttle settings are now standard in the major sims. Uncrashed 3.0 aligned throttle mid, expo and hover behaviour "to match the latest Betaflight". TRYP added "Betaflight-style" switch management. FPV Logic offers Actual, Raceflight and KISS rates. A 2026 benchmark ran Actual rates identically in Liftoff, Uncrashed and TRYP.

### Cited Findings

**General expert guidance (Oscar Liang, Aug 2026)** — [Oscar Liang](https://oscarliang.com/fpv-simulator/)
- On gamepads and keyboards: "Pro Tip: I don't recommend using gaming consoles (such as PlayStation or Xbox) or a keyboard, as skills gained from these methods rarely translate to flying a real FPV drone using an actual radio controller. And they don't offer enough control precision and actually make flying much harder to learn."
- On radios: "All the radios I recommended are plug-and-play with the simulators on this page: just plug in the USB cable, and your radio will appear as a gaming joystick on your computer." and "If you have a DJI Remote Controller (such as RC2 or RC3), it's also plug-and-play with these simulators."
- On settings: "the default settings in most FPV sims typically aren't optimal" and "One important setting to customize early is your rates".
- On performance and "floatiness": "When Liftoff first came out, I initially complained it felt floaty and unresponsive. It turned out my PC was simply too slow." Low frame rates cause "stuttering, frame drops, and increased latency".
- On trying before buying: Steam's "return policy, allowing refunds for games played for less than two hours, ideal for testing a simulator".
- Bardwell's chapter title: "If you play sims, you MUST be using EdgeTX!!!" The description says EdgeTX 2.5 brings "the latency fix for your USB controller". — [YouTube I7lUTEJM62g](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I7lUTEJM62g)

### Inferences
- **"RC radio required?"** For the dedicated sims the answer is no, but experts strongly recommend one. Several titles block keyboard-only play (Liftoff, Uncrashed, The Zone, FPV Logic: "Only playable with a RC transmitter or joystick"). For a Call of Duty player starting at home, a gamepad works to try a sim, but every expert source points to a USB radio for transferable skill.
- **Several expert physics verdicts predate big physics updates**, so a 2026 tier list may legitimately differ:
  - Uncrashed Physics 3.0 came out on 21 Oct 2026, after Oscar's August review.
  - TRYP's aerodynamic reworks (Dec 2025 and Jun 2026) may or may not be reflected in Oscar's Aug 2026 text.
  - Micro Drones moved to Physics 6.0 on 1 Jun 2026.
- **Oscar's USD prices are partly stale:**
  - Micro Drones: "From $5.99", but Steam US shows $15.99.
  - TRYP: $14.99, but Steam US shows $16.99.
  - So his Tiny Whoop GO reasoning ("nearly three times higher than Liftoff: Micro Drones") was probably made against an older or discounted Micro Drones price. At today's prices, about $15 for TWG is roughly level with Micro Drones' $15.99.
  - This is my inference from [Steam US price API](https://store.steampowered.com/api/appdetails?appids=1432320,1881200&cc=us&filters=price_overview) against Oscar's page.
- **Performance matters for a beginner's choice.** Liftoff's Steam minimum is an RTX 2060-class GPU; Uncrashed and TRYP are called demanding; VelociDrone, Freerider and CurryKitten run on modest PCs. Bodycam needs an RTX 2070-class GPU.

### Gaps
- I could not verify from primary sources which rate systems VelociDrone, DRL, Micro Drones and Liftoff currently offer. The Liftoff information is from 2021 plus a 2026 YouTuber's test bench.
- Tiny Whoop GO's current price and availability are unverified.
- Propwash's Modrinth page was not found through the API: project lookups returned 404 and search found nothing. Its price is not stated; the developer says it is distributed on CurseForge and Modrinth.
- No source measured sim physics objectively, apart from developer claims (The Zone) and a small YouTuber benchmark whose results were not retrievable.
