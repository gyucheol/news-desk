## Q1. What established FPV educators and manufacturers recommend as the first radio in 2025–2026, and why

### Takeaway
Oscar Liang's beginner radio guide (dated 10 Aug 2026) names the RadioMaster Pocket "Cheapest Worth Having": cheap, EdgeTX + ExpressLRS built in, simulator-ready, but with "small gimbal size and limited RF output power". The guide's other picks:
- RadioMaster Boxer: "Best Value and Versatility".
- Jumper T20S: portability.
- RadioMaster TX15: colour screen.
- DJI Remote Controller 3: only for pilots who fly DJI O4 exclusively.

His Feb 2026 bundles guide says to start with a simulator plus a T8L or Pocket, and calls most ready-to-fly kits poor value. Joshua Bardwell's current shopping list (changelog to 10 Nov 2026) lists only mid- and high-end RadioMaster radios plus the new GX10 Max. His Pocket video's title says it is "Perfect For Three Specific People", but I could not read its content.

The consensus criteria are ExpressLRS (ELRS), EdgeTX, Hall gimbals and USB-joystick simulator support. The Pocket meets all four at the lowest price.

### Inferences

**Decision summary for this user** (sim-first at home, 1–2 h weeknights, may fly an indoor whoop later; budget, hand size and CoD platform unknown):

1. **Default pick: Pocket ELRS (not CC2500) plus two 18650 cells.**
   - It is the only radio named by both of Oscar's 2026 beginner guides.
   - It has Hall gimbals, EdgeTX, a USB-joystick mode and ELRS, so it binds later to current ELRS whoops (Q4).
   - The official list price is $71.50 plus $11.99 for RadioMaster's two-cell pack. That is low enough that a later upgrade wastes little.
   - Its gamepad shape suits thumb-style grip, which Oscar links to compact radios. That may feel familiar if the user plays CoD on a console pad (untested hypothesis).
2. **Better if the budget allows and the user expects to stay in the hobby, pinches the sticks, or has large hands: Boxer ELRS (or Boxer Crush).**
   - It has full-size Hall gimbals, 1 W ELRS and long battery life, and Oscar says "it's worth investing a bit more". It is Oscar's Best Value pick, and Bardwell lists the Boxer Crush.
   - The cost is weight: 638 g with cells, versus about 380 g for the Pocket (Q2).
3. **Better if the user wants a gamepad shape with bigger (mini) gimbals and a colour screen: RadioMaster GX10.**
   - It is the new Zorro successor: 2×18650, colour touchscreen, pre-order shipping early December 2026.
   - It has no reviews yet, and availability in Korea is unknown.
   - The Zorro itself is weakened by its 18350 cells: Oscar measured 1.5–2 h runtime on the ELRS version, short against 1–2 h practice sessions.
4. **Cheapest workable option: RadioMaster T8L ($34.99).** It has Hall nano gimbals and works with sims, but has no screen and runs a fixed T8L firmware rather than EdgeTX. Oscar rates the Pocket "the more capable radio".
5. **Only if the user is committed to DJI** (Neo 2 / Avata 2 / DJI-goggle O4 whoops) and will never fly analog or ELRS-only quads: **DJI FPV Remote Controller 3**. This is Oscar's "For Dedicated DJI Users" pick.
6. **Not recommended as this user's first radio:**
   - TBS Tango 2: Crossfire rather than ELRS, high price, patchy stock, and dropped from Bardwell's list.
   - Jumper T-Pro / T15: neither is a current pick in Oscar's guide.
   - Jumper T-Lite V2: older, and listed as discontinued.
   - LiteRadio 3/4: potentiometer gimbals (LR4 confirmed by maker), BETAFPV-specific firmware, and the LR3 AMD-PC caveat. Fine as a kit-bundled radio, not the best standalone buy (Q5).

## Q2. Side-by-side comparison of the candidates

### Takeaway
On paper the Pocket ELRS gives the most for the least money among radios with Hall gimbals, EdgeTX and ELRS:
- nano Hall gimbals
- 250 mW ELRS (FCC firmware) or 100 mW (EU LBT firmware)
- 2×18650 cells, not included
- 288 g without cells
- $71.50 list

Its trade-offs against the alternatives:
- **Boxer:** full-size gimbals, 1 W, much heavier.
- **Zorro and TX12 MKII:** larger mini gimbals; the Zorro has weak 18350 battery life.
- **GX10:** newer gamepad with a colour screen.
- **T8L:** cheaper and screenless.
- **LiteRadio 4:** cheaper, built-in battery, but potentiometer gimbals.
- **DJI RC3:** built-in battery, but locked to DJI.
- **Tango 2:** Crossfire, expensive.

## Q3. Known weaknesses of the Pocket for a beginner and how serious they are

### Takeaway
Reviewers name four weak points:
- small nano gimbals and short sticks
- modest RF power
- 18650 cells sold separately, with some reports of cells not fitting
- some cheap-feeling switches and trim buttons

Separately, EdgeTX has a learning curve that matters for any EdgeTX radio. None of the four is treated as disqualifying for a budget beginner:
- Oscar still calls it "an excellent choice for those just starting out… with a tight budget".
- Eleccelerator says it "will be great for casual flying".

Ranked for this user, the realistic concerns are: (1) first-time EdgeTX/ELRS setup, (2) gimbal size if the user has large hands or pinches, (3) buying cells that fit. RF power doesn't matter for sims or indoor whoops.

## Q4. Future compatibility: ELRS whoops, DJI O4 whoops, Avata 2 / Neo 2, and what ELRS vs DJI means for this user
