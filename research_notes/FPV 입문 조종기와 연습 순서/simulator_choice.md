## 1. Learning structure: tutorials, training, flight school, and Korean support

### Takeaway
- **Liftoff** has the most complete beginner path inside the base price (₩25,000). Since Jan 2023 it has had an interactive, step-by-step in-game tutorial "from takeoff to landing", and Oscar Liang calls its training "an excellent structured learning path for complete beginners". Some players dislike how the tutorial is organised.
- **FPV SkyDive** is free. Since May 2026 it has a new guided intro: calibration, a third-person view and mini exercises. Its structured curriculum is split:
  - Flight School Unit 1 (angle mode) is free.
  - Units 2–5, which include the acro lessons, are a **₩12,500 DLC**.
- **TRYP** has a "first fly guide" tutorial and challenges graded by difficulty. However, its own developer warned in Dec 2023 that the tutorial "is difficult and will be reworked", and Oscar says its training "falls short".
- **None of the three offers Korean** interface text or subtitles on Steam.

### Inferences
- A Korean beginner gets English-only instruction in all three. SkyDive's third-person "see how the drone reacts" intro and Liftoff's step-by-step tutorial are visual, so the language barrier is moderate rather than blocking. This is my judgement, not tested.
- **SkyDive's free path now covers calibration, mini exercises (throttle, yaw, pitch, roll) and angle-mode Unit 1.** Structured acro teaching in SkyDive is paid (Unit 2 onward).
- On price for learning content (computed): SkyDive Flight School is ₩12,500. Liftoff is ₩25,000 and includes its tutorial plus the full simulator. For a PC that can run Liftoff, the Flight School DLC is hard to justify. It makes sense mainly on weaker PCs.
- TRYP is the weakest of the three for structured learning. Its tutorial carried a developer "difficult" warning, I found no later rework announcement beyond a Jun 2025 hover-step tweak, and expert opinion is negative.

## 2. Flight physics and realism for learning acro: expert verdicts and newer updates (dated)

### Takeaway
- Oscar Liang's guide (29 Aug 2026) still labels:
  - Liftoff "Best for beginners", with physics that "can feel slightly 'floaty'";
  - TRYP best for cinematic flying, with quads "slightly heavier than in real life" but physics "reasonably accurate";
  - SkyDive best free, with "mediocre" physics.
- All three developers have shipped physics work recently: Liftoff **Physics 6.0** (public 10 Feb 2026), TRYP's aerodynamic reworks (18 Dec 2025 and 2 Jun 2026), and SkyDive's data-driven "Realistic System" (Dec 2025–Mar 2026). I cannot verify whether Oscar re-tested after each one.
- TRYP's developer still says its physics are not "1.0".
- **Liftoff 2.0** (experimental branch since 6 Nov 2026) is a UI and track-builder update, not a physics change.

### Inferences
- For a first-timer, the "floaty" (Liftoff) versus "heavy" (TRYP) difference matters less than three other things: having a structured path, a smooth frame rate, and consistent practice. Oscar's own "consistent practice" line and his PC-too-slow anecdote support this.
- Liftoff's "floaty" label may be partly dated. Physics 6.0 (Feb 2026) postdates most "floaty" complaints, and Oscar's Liftoff con text may predate it. Treat "floaty" as a caveat, not a disqualifier.
- TRYP's physics are still changing: WIP propwash in Jun 2026, and "not 1.0" by its own account. That makes it a weaker anchor for a beginner's first muscle memory, but a reasonable later add-on.
- SkyDive's "mediocre" verdict has not been re-confirmed after its 2025–26 "Realistic System". It should be treated as current expert opinion, possibly a little dated.

### Gaps
- I found no independent, named, 2026 hands-on comparison of Liftoff Physics 6.0, TRYP after Jun 2026 and SkyDive's Realistic System.
- Oscar does not date each simulator paragraph separately, so it is unknown which of his physics verdicts were re-tested in 2026.

## 3. Practicality: Steam Korea price, free vs DLC, PC specs, Windows/Mac, radio setup, rates and camera settings, tiny whoops, multiplayer

### Takeaway
- **Prices on Steam Korea (2026-11-11 11:48 UTC), no discounts running:**
  - FPV SkyDive: free. DLC: Flight School ₩12,500, Micro Drone Edition ₩11,000, Drone Soccer ₩8,900, Track Builder ₩6,700.
  - Liftoff: ₩25,000.
  - TRYP: ₩18,500 (Early Access, no DLC).
  - Liftoff: Micro Drones: ₩17,500.
- **Hardware:**
  - SkyDive is by far the lightest: GTX 650 minimum, 2 GB of storage.
  - Liftoff needs an **RTX 2060 / RX Vega 56-class GPU even at minimum** and does not support Intel integrated graphics.
  - TRYP's minimum is a GTX 960, but it needs DirectX 12 and Windows 10+, recommends 16 GB RAM, and has optimisation complaints.
- **Platforms:** all three run on Windows and Mac. Liftoff and TRYP accept Apple Silicon M1 as a minimum. Linux is supported by Liftoff and SkyDive only.
- **Controls:** all three accept a USB radio as a joystick and let you adjust rates and camera angle.
- **Tiny whoops:** SkyDive sells them as paid DLC. Liftoff needs the separate Micro Drones sim. TRYP has none yet.
- **Multiplayer:** Liftoff has full online multiplayer, SkyDive has multiplayer, co-op and LAN, and TRYP is single-player with multiplayer in beta.

## 5. Recommended order for a beginner with 1–2 hours on weeknights

### Takeaway
The sources support a simple path, in this order:
1. Free **SkyDive** for the first session: check that the radio is detected over USB, run the new calibration intro and mini exercises, and see whether acro clicks.
2. **Liftoff as the one main sim** if the PC meets the RTX 2060-class bar. This is the first pick of both Oscar Liang and Unmanned Tech. Do its tutorial, then about 10 focused hours.
3. Only afterwards, optional add-ons: **Liftoff: Micro Drones** before buying or flying an indoor tiny whoop, and **TRYP** for cinematic or open-world freestyle once basic control is solid.
4. If the PC is too weak for Liftoff, stay on SkyDive. Consider its Flight School DLC (₩12,500) for the acro lessons.
- Unmanned Tech warns against installing everything: pick one and practise.

## 6. Simple skill milestones for knowing when to move on

### Takeaway
2026 guides largely agree on a ladder:
1. Open map; arm and disarm; take off and land gently with throttle control.
2. Broad circuits and turns.
3. Figure-eights in both directions at a constant height.
4. Flying through large gaps or gates.
5. Deliberate recovery from bad entries.
6. A planned short line or circuit repeated reliably.
- Move on when you can repeat a task "without relying on reset". About 10 hours is "a useful minimum, not a licence".
- The sims' own structures follow a similar order:
  - SkyDive's mini exercises and its Flight School units (basics → acro → advanced → manoeuvres → tricks);
  - TRYP's challenges graded "Rookie to Expert";
  - Liftoff's tutorial ("from takeoff to landing").

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
**Milestone checklist for the report** (my synthesis of the cited guides):
- **M1:** arm, take off, hold a low hover, land, and disarm without a crash on an open map.
- **M2:** broad circuits with coordinated turns at a steady height.
- **M3:** figure-eights in both directions, without resets.
- **M4:** clean passes through large gaps or gates.
- **M5:** recover from a botched entry.
- **M6:** repeat a planned 3–4-feature line or a simple lap consistently without resets. Then add a second sim (Micro Drones or TRYP) or move to real hardware.
- On the schedule in §5, M1–M3 plausibly fit in the first week and M4–M6 in the second. This is an inference; individual pace varies.
