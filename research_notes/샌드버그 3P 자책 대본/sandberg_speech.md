## Q2. Backstory: Dave Goldberg's death and the 30-day (sheloshim) Facebook post

### Takeaway
Dave Goldberg, SurveyMonkey's CEO, died at 47 on **Friday, May 1, 2015**, in the resort area of **Punta Mita (Nayarit state, Mexico)**, near Puerto Vallarta. A Mexican state official first said he slipped on a treadmill and died of head trauma and blood loss. Sandberg later said he died **"in seconds from a cardiac arrhythmia"** caused by **coronary artery disease his doctors had not identified**. At the end of the 30-day mourning period (sheloshim), on June 3, 2015, she posted an essay on Facebook. That essay was the first place she told both the 3 P's idea (which she credited there to Adam Grant) and the Option A / Option B story.

## Q3. The 3 P's: what she said about each P, and how she applied it to grief and to everyday failures

### Takeaway
She presented the three P's (**personalization, pervasiveness, permanence**) as Martin Seligman's findings on "how we bounce back from hardship." For each P she gave a definition, then an example from her grief, then a humorous everyday example (a spreadsheet blunder, breakups, her divorce). For a 자책 (self-blame) theme, the core lines are "not everything that happens to us happens because of us," and the distinction she drew between blaming yourself and taking responsibility.

### Cited Findings
All quotes in this section are from the [UC Berkeley official text (PDF)](https://news.berkeley.edu/wp-content/uploads/2016/05/Sheryl-Sandberg-Berkeley-commencement-speech.pdf). The identical text is at [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2016/05/14/sandberg-uc-berkley-transcript/).

**Framing**
- "As a representative of Silicon Valley, I'm pleased to tell you there is data to learn from. After spending decades studying how people deal with setbacks, psychologist Martin Seligman found that there are three P's—personalization, pervasiveness, and permanence—that are critical to how we bounce back from hardship. The seeds of resilience are planted in the way we process the negative events in our lives."
  - KO: "심리학자 마틴 셀리그먼은 사람들이 좌절을 다루는 방식을 수십 년간 연구한 끝에, 역경에서 다시 일어서는 데 결정적인 세 가지 P(개인화, 만연성, 영속성)를 찾아냈습니다. 회복탄력성의 씨앗은 우리가 삶의 부정적인 사건을 받아들이는 방식에 심겨 있습니다."

**P1, personalization (the 자책 core)**
- **Definition:** "The first P is personalization—the belief that we are at fault. This is different from taking responsibility, which you should always do. This is the lesson that not everything that happens to us happens because of us."
  - KO: "첫 번째 P는 개인화, 즉 '내 잘못'이라는 믿음입니다. 우리에게 일어나는 모든 일이 우리 때문에 일어나는 것은 아닙니다."
- **Applied to her grief:** "When Dave died, I had a very common reaction, which was to blame myself. He died in seconds from a cardiac arrhythmia. I poured [sic] over his medical records asking what I could have—or should have—done. It wasn't until I learned about the three P's that I accepted that I could not have prevented his death. His doctors had not identified his coronary artery disease. I was an economics major; how could I have?"
  - KO: "데이브가 세상을 떠났을 때 저는 아주 흔한 반응, 바로 저 자신을 탓하는 반응을 보였습니다. 의사들도 발견하지 못한 관상동맥질환을 경제학 전공자인 제가 어떻게 알 수 있었겠어요?"
  - Note: the official text spells it "poured," not "pored." Correct it or mark it [sic] if the line appears on screen.
- **The research she pointed to:** "Studies show that getting past personalization can actually make you stronger. Teachers who knew they could do better after students failed adjusted their methods and saw future classes go on to excel. College swimmers who underperformed but believed they were capable of swimming faster did. Not taking failures personally allows us to recover—and even to thrive."
  - KO: "실패를 개인의 탓으로 받아들이지 않을 때, 우리는 회복할 수 있고 오히려 더 크게 성장할 수도 있습니다."
- **Everyday example:** "And I wish I had understood personalization when boyfriends broke up with me. Sometimes it's not you—it really is them. I mean, that dude never showered."
  - KO: "남자친구들에게 차였을 때 개인화를 알았더라면 좋았을 거예요. 때로는 당신 탓이 아니라, 정말 상대 탓이거든요."

**P2, pervasiveness**
- **Definition:** "The second P is pervasiveness—the belief that an event will affect all areas of your life. You know that song 'Everything is awesome?' This is the flip: 'Everything is awful.' There's no place to run or hide from the all-consuming sadness."
  - KO: "두 번째 P는 만연성, 즉 한 사건이 삶의 모든 영역에 영향을 미칠 거라는 믿음입니다. '모든 게 끝내줘'라는 노래를 뒤집은 '모든 게 끔찍해'인 셈이죠."
- **Applied to her grief:** "The child psychologists I spoke to encouraged me to get my kids back to their routine as soon as possible. So ten days after Dave died, they went back to school and I went back to work. I remember sitting in my first Facebook meeting in a deep, deep haze… But then I got drawn into the discussion and for a second—a brief split second—I forgot about death."
  - KO: "그런데 어느새 논의에 빠져들었고, 아주 짧은 한순간 죽음을 잊었습니다."
- **What that moment showed her:** "That brief second helped me see that there were other things in my life that were not awful. My children and I were healthy. My friends and family were so loving and they carried us—quite literally at times."
  - KO: "그 짧은 순간 덕분에 제 삶에 끔찍하지 않은 다른 것들도 있다는 걸 알게 됐습니다."
- **Acknowledging her privilege, inside the pervasiveness section:** "The loss of a partner often has severe negative financial consequences, especially for women… I had financial security, the ability to take the time off I needed, and a job that I did not just believe in…"
  - KO: "배우자를 잃는 일은 특히 여성에게 심각한 경제적 타격이 되곤 합니다. 저에게는 경제적 안정과, 필요한 만큼 쉴 수 있는 여건이 있었습니다."
- **Everyday example:** "Day one of my first job out of college, my boss found out that I didn't know how to enter data into Lotus 1-2-3… I went home convinced that I was going to be fired. I thought I was terrible at everything… but it turns out I was only terrible at spreadsheets. Understanding pervasiveness would have saved me a lot of anxiety that week."
  - KO: "저는 제가 모든 일에 형편없다고 생각했어요. 그런데 알고 보니 스프레드시트에만 형편없었던 거죠."

**P3, permanence**
- **Definition:** "The third P is permanence—the belief that the sorrow will last forever. For months, no matter what I did, it felt like the crushing grief would always be there."
  - KO: "세 번째 P는 영속성, 즉 이 슬픔이 영원히 계속될 거라는 믿음입니다. 몇 달 동안 무엇을 해도 이 짓누르는 슬픔이 언제까지나 있을 것만 같았습니다."
- **The "second derivative" of feelings:** "We often project our current feelings out indefinitely—and experience what I think of as the second derivative of those feelings. We feel anxious—and then we feel anxious that we're anxious. We feel sad—and then we feel sad that we're sad. Instead, we should accept our feelings—but recognize that they will not last forever."
  - KO: "우리는 불안해하고, 그다음엔 불안하다는 사실 때문에 또 불안해합니다. 감정은 받아들이되, 그 감정이 영원히 가지는 않는다는 걸 알아야 합니다."
- **Her rabbi's advice:** "My rabbi told me that time would heal but for now I should 'lean in to the suck.' It was good advice, but not really what I meant by 'lean in.'" This is a joke on her book *Lean In*, and "suck" is mild slang.
- **Everyday example:** "I wish I had known about permanence when I broke up with boyfriends. It would've been a comfort to know that feeling was not going to last forever, and if I was being honest with myself… neither were any of those relationships."
  - KO: "그 감정이 영원히 가지 않는다는 걸 알았다면 위로가 됐을 거예요. 솔직히 말하면, 그 연애들도 영원하진 않았지만요."

**All three P's at once**
- "And all three P's ganged up on me in my twenties after my first marriage ended in divorce. I thought at the time that no matter what I accomplished, I was a massive failure."
  - KO: "20대에 첫 결혼이 이혼으로 끝났을 때, 세 가지 P가 한꺼번에 저를 덮쳤습니다. 그때 저는 무엇을 이루든 저는 완전한 실패자라고 생각했어요."

**How to use the 3 P's**
- "The three P's are common emotional reactions to so many things that happen to us—in our careers, our personal lives, and our relationships. You're probably feeling one of them right now about something in your life. But if you can recognize you are falling into these traps, you can catch yourself.
