# Commencement (graduation) speeches: most viewed, most cited, recurring themes, the 2024–2026 trend, and Korean coverage (checked 2026-11-06)

## Q1. Which commencement speeches have the highest YouTube views or appear most often in "best ever" rankings? (all-time)

### Takeaway
On official uploads, Steve Jobs' Stanford 2005 speech leads by a wide margin (49.4M views on Stanford's channel). Next come Adm. McRaven's UT Austin 2014 "Make your bed" speech (31.5M across two university-affiliated uploads) and Jim Carrey's MUM/MIU 2014 speech (17.4M official). Counting third-party re-uploads, McRaven (about 100M across the uploads listed) and Denzel Washington, whose speeches are mostly circulated as motivational re-cuts (40M+ for one "Put God First" re-upload), rival or pass Jobs. David Foster Wallace's "This Is Water" is the main outlier: it ranks near the top of "best ever" lists, yet no official video exists and its re-uploads reach only about 1.6–2.7M views each.

### Cited Findings
**Method and caveats (applies to every count below)**
- All counts were checked on **2026-11-06, 07:32–07:40 UTC** through YouTube's InnerTube `search` endpoint (sorted by view count, `params:"CAM="`), with exact counts and upload dates confirmed through the `next` endpoint. The YouTube watch-page HTML returned `LOGIN_REQUIRED` (bot gate), so it was not used. Counts are the exact numbers YouTube displays. "Official" means the institution's or the speaker's own channel; "affiliated" means e.g. an alumni association or a university magazine; anything else is a "re-upload". Each count links to the video: `[YT](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ID)`.
- In Korean-locale searches (`hl=ko`), YouTube shows **auto-translated titles** on English uploads. For example, Harvard's own Rowling upload appears as "J.K. 롤링 하버드 대학교 졸업식 연설". Korean uploads were therefore identified by channel and description language, not by title — [YT](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UibfDUPJAEU).

By contrast, Zuckerberg (7.3M), Sacha Baron Cohen (12.2M) and Mark Rober (11.6M) have high views but appear on few "best ever" lists (Q4).

## Q2. Which themes recur, and which are psychology-relevant (meaning, resilience, self-compassion, habits, mortality salience, gratitude)?

### Takeaway
Across NPR's 350+ speech database and three "best ever" lists, the most common commencement themes are: change the world / purpose; embrace failure / don't give up; follow your inner voice or dream; work hard; be kind. The most-viewed speeches cluster on failure and resilience (Rowling, Conan, Denzel, Oprah, Sandberg, Federer, Swift), mortality and meaning (Jobs), small disciplined habits (McRaven), fear versus love (Carrey), attention and awareness (DFW), and, increasingly from 2024 to 2026, humility versus narcissism, luck, perfectionism, and AI-era anxiety. Almost every one of these maps directly onto a mainstream psychology construct.

### Inferences
- **The 2024–2026 thematic shift.** Classic speeches centered on following your passion, failure, and mortality. Recent viral speeches center on (a) **humility and anti-narcissism** and openness to being wrong (Conan 2026, the Harvard student, Jonny Kim's "no solo superhero"); (b) **AI-era career anxiety answered with effort, agency and responsibility** (Huang 2023, 2024 and 2026; Su 2026; Garber 2026); and (c) **imperfection and process over outcome** (Federer's 54%). All three map onto psychology content: intellectual humility, uncertainty tolerance, and self-compassion.
- **Context drives virality.** Political conflict (Harvard vs. the Trump administration; campus protests in 2024) produced the largest third-party view spikes. Headline framing ("SLAMS Trump", "Fiery Speech") outperformed the official uploads by roughly 2–6×.
- For a Korean audience, **Jonny Kim** (a Korean-American astronaut) and **Federer** look like high-potential, low-competition subjects (inference from the Q5 search results).

## Q5. Which of these were widely translated or covered in Korea (high-view Korean-subtitled uploads, Korean news)?

### Takeaway
On Korean YouTube the most-translated foreign commencement speeches are Conan O'Brien's Dartmouth 2011 address (four Korean-subtitled uploads totaling about 7.7M views), Rick Rigsby (POKEPOKE, 6.46M), McRaven (POKEPOKE, 2.45M; plus the Korean book 『침대부터 정리하라』), Jobs (Korean-subtitle uploads of 2.08M and 1.66M), Denzel (three uploads of 1.2–1.4M) and Zuckerberg (1.1M). Korea's own viral commencement speeches (June Huh SNU 2022, Lee Hyori Kookmin 2024, Bang Si-hyuk SNU 2019) match or beat most foreign ones domestically. Federer, Rowling, DFW, Sandberg and Saunders are comparatively under-served.

### Inferences
- **Korean-subtitle demand is concentrated, not proportional to US fame.** Conan Dartmouth's Korean re-uploads (≈7.7M) equal about 75% of its two official uploads (≈10.3M). Rigsby and Holtz (little-known in Korea) reached 6.5M and 3.6M through POKEPOKE's emotional, short-edit format. Meanwhile Federer (≈14K), Boseman (≈10K full version), McConaughey (≈46K) and DFW (≤0.35M) are thin. That leaves a gap a psychology channel could fill with an analytic, rather than purely motivational, framing.
- **Proven Korean formats:**
  - Short emotional edits with a punchline title (POKEPOKE, 필미필미TV).
  - Full Korean–English subtitled versions for English learners (올뉴씽킹, Papa English, "영어로 잠들기").
  - "You really have to watch this" re-uploads that took off in 2026 (인간지능).
  - Broadcaster summaries with a political hook (SBS on Conan 2026).
- **Korean audiences respond to self-compassion and anti-competition messages** (June Huh's "be kind to yourself", Choi Jae-chun's "what's the point of living well alone", Lee Hyori's "do as you please"). That aligns well with an INFJ psychology channel. Pairing a foreign classic (e.g., DFW or Sandberg) with a Korean counterpart (June Huh) could be a strong format.
- **Content-risk flags:** Neil Gaiman ("Make Good Art") faces sexual-assault allegations (NY Magazine, Jan 2025), which he denies — [Salon](https://www.salon.com/2025/01/15/im-not-willing-to-turn-my-back-on-the-truth-neil-gaiman-denies-allegations); [ABC News/AP](https://abcnews.go.com/Entertainment/wireStory/british-author-neil-gaiman-denies-engaging-consensual-sex-117688542). Harrison Butker's speech is politically and religiously polarizing — [YT/CNN](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2ARjgzfcpOo). The 2026 Harvard speeches are framed in Korea through Trump politics ("트럼프 SPECIAL"). A psychology channel may prefer to foreground their universal themes: humility, narcissism, openness.
