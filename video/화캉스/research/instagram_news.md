## 4. Patterns

**What got traction (only where numbers were seen):**
1. **"Permission" plus a mental-health framing beats plain jokes.** The biggest reported short-form numbers are a TikTok PSA ("could be a trauma response", 1.6M) and the Korean-reported 972만 bathroom-camping video. Queensland Health's "It's okay to poo at work" reel got >22K IG likes in a week. The hook says "you're not weird / it's okay," then gives a reason (stress, trauma, health).
2. **Meme template + serious twist.** Queensland Health put a trending psychedelic meme format on top of health information (parcopresis, haemorrhoids).
3. **Money-math humor.** The "Boss makes a dollar" rhyme has run since 2009; its biggest single post was 116K likes. Variants include the 10 min/day = 40 hrs/year macro, poop-salary calculators, and the Blind "김부장 화캉스 47%" tally. "You're being paid right now" is the core 월급루팡 joke.
4. **Local familiarity.** 변기걸즈's reels passed 1M because viewers know the bathrooms. Office reels by small brand accounts hit 4–5M through a metaphor (cats = dev vs biz team) or 공감 skits.
5. **News pegs come in waves.** The Korean 화캉스 news burst (한경 7/14 → 매모판 7/15 → 르데스크 7/16 → MBC 7/17, 2025) came directly after US bathroom-camping coverage (Jul 5–10). Korean media framed 화캉스 as the K-version of a US Gen-Z trend, even though Blind users had used the word since 2021.

**Angles that repeat:**
- The bathroom as the "only private space" (privacy zone, control, restoration).
- Guilt and 월급루팡 humor.
- Rush hours (right after arriving, before leaving; "항상 만석").
- Napping, Shorts, stock checking.
- Employer surveillance and crackdowns (notices, ID scans, photos, fines, sloped toilets).
- Health warnings (5-minute rule, haemorrhoids).
- Partner/household conflict (husband 화캉스, weaponized pooping).
- The bathroom as the place to cry (사람인 2008: 59.7%).

**What's missing (gaps the video could fill):**
- **No recent Korean survey**: nothing on how many workers take 화캉스, how long, or what they do on their phones. The best Korean data is 2014 (25.7% bathroom hideout) and 2008 (59.7% cried there). The only numeric trend signal is 퀘타아이's +94.13% SNS buzz.
- **No verified Korean Instagram reel on 화캉스 with counts.** Korean IG content in this space is mostly cartoon/meme accounts and brand skits. There seems to be no breakout "POV: 화캉스" reel, or at least none indexable.
- **Psychology beyond one-liners**: restorative-environment theory (only the brunch HR essay mentions it), the micro-break literature, and the "backstage" idea. Also why smokers' breaks (담타) are tolerated but quiet breaks are not.
- **Women's side**: bathroom anxiety and 화장실 눈치 are big in UK/US surveys (women 48% vs men 19% scared to poop at work) but nearly absent from Korean 화캉스 coverage, which skews to men and husbands.
- **Employer and design solutions** (quiet rooms, nap pods, break norms) vs policing. Only notices and crackdowns were found.
